FAITH DOES NOT FIT IN A CENSUS CHECKBOX.

Brazil's statistics agency counts religious identities. Thankfully, it does not pretend to count who lives by them.

The Crow Investigator · Raven Crow · 2026-11-21

Brazil counted its faith with the precision of someone weighing beans. In the 2022 census, 56.7% of people aged ten or older identified as Catholic, down from 65.1% in 2010. That matters. But there is a peculiar temptation to read the figure as a medical report on the nation's soul. Census takers ask about religion. They do not ask whether someone forgave a sibling, visited the sick or lied to win an argument.

Figures released by Brazil's statistics agency, IBGE, in June 2025 also showed 26.9% identifying as evangelical Christians and 9.3% as having no religion in that age group. These are measures of religious identification, not of virtue. Someone may tick 'Catholic' without ever entering a church; someone else may profess no religion and show a generosity that would embarrass many regular churchgoers. The questionnaire is useful. It has simply not been granted the power to read consciences.

I am Catholic, and I do not regard the decline in the Catholic share as good news for the Church. But blaming modern life, screens or the neighbor who joined another congregation would be far too convenient. The uncomfortable question is what kind of witness we offer. If faith appears only when it helps win a political quarrel, no wonder outsiders mistake the Gospel for a Sunday election committee.

Some see these figures as a healthy advance in individual freedom, and they have a point. Nobody should inherit a belief as though it were a utility bill issued in their name. Article 5 of Brazil's Constitution protects freedom of conscience and belief. It protects both the practice of religion and the choice to profess none. Coerced faith may fill a register; that does not make it conviction.

The opposite mistake is to announce that religion no longer matters because its statistical share has fallen. The roughly one hundred million people identifying as Catholic in 2022, according to IBGE, are no footnote; nor does that figure entitle anyone to speak for each of them. Growth in other traditions deserves understanding, not the treatment reserved for a sports rivalry. Turn a country into a league table of churches and you may celebrate points while forgetting people.

I am not asking IBGE to add a holiness column to its questionnaire. It would be the first survey in which everyone became suspicious of their own mother. I am asking us not to confuse affiliation with practice, a majority with truth, or freedom with indifference. A faith that needs percentages to justify itself has already begun looking in a chart for what it ought to show in life.
