Dollar closes at R$5.586, a 15-month high, after US inflation tops forecasts

The US currency gains 1.46% while Brazil's Ibovespa falls 0.52%, as US inflation raises rate expectations.

What happened

On November 10, 2026, the US dollar closed at R$5.5860 in Brazil, up 1.46% and at its highest closing level since July 31, 2025. It reached R$5.6135 intraday. The Ibovespa fell 0.52% to 171,336.99 points. US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above a forecast of 0.3%, increasing expectations of a Federal Reserve interest-rate rise. In Brazil, investors also assessed the minutes of the Copom (Brazil's Monetary Policy Committee), which highlighted inflation and fiscal risks. Agência Brasil and other Brazilian outlets reported the closing figures.

The Crow's analysis

A stronger dollar can raise the cost of imports, inputs and dollar-linked contracts, depending on how long the move lasts and whether businesses pass costs on. Higher US interest rates can make dollar assets more attractive relative to emerging-market currencies. Brazil's fiscal outlook and expectations of sustained high domestic interest rates also affect sentiment. A single trading day, however, does not establish a long-term exchange-rate trend.

Counterpoint

The move cannot be attributed solely to Brazilian domestic policy: stronger-than-expected US inflation and changes in US interest-rate expectations were significant international factors. Nor does one closing price establish that the currency will stay at that level or that all costs will immediately reach consumers.
