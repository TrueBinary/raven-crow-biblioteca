Brazil's Enem 2026 essay focuses on valuing universal public healthcare

Education minister announces essay prompt on healthcare as a universal right; more than five million people registered.

What happened

The essay topic for Brazil's Exame Nacional do Ensino Médio (Enem, national secondary education examination) in 2026, held on Sunday, November 8, is “Valuing the Unified Health System (SUS) as a universal right.” Education Minister Leonardo Barchini announced the prompt, which independent news organizations confirmed. The first day includes an essay, 45 Language questions and 45 Humanities questions. According to the Instituto Nacional de Estudos e Pesquisas Educacionais Anísio Teixeira (Inep, Brazil's education assessment institute), 5,051,345 people registered. Candidates must write an argumentative essay defending a thesis and proposing an intervention that respects human rights. The second exam day, covering Mathematics and Natural Sciences, is scheduled for November 15. The announcement does not mean all candidates have finished the test.

The Crow's analysis

The topic invites candidates to examine the gap between a constitutional right to healthcare and actual access to services. They may discuss regional disparities in appointments and treatment, funding and management challenges, vaccination, primary care and prevention. Recognizing the achievements of public healthcare does not prevent a critical examination of its shortcomings. A strong essay depends on coherent reasoning, relevant evidence and a credible proposal.

Counterpoint

Education specialists say the topic is approachable but generic praise for the SUS is no substitute for a reasoned argument. Waiting lists, unequal access and resource management are legitimate concerns. Grading does not require a particular party-political position; it assesses writing, argumentation and respect for human rights.
