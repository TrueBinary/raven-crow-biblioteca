Saudi Arabia says it intercepted three missiles and a drone in new attacks blamed on Houthis

Coalition says two projectiles were destroyed near Riyadh and another in the south, as authorities issued public warnings.

What happened

On November 10, 2026, the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted and destroyed three missiles and a drone launched by Yemen's Houthi movement. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki said a ballistic missile and a cruise missile were aimed at the Riyadh region, another ballistic missile was intercepted in southern Saudi Arabia, and a drone was brought down over the Eastern Province. Civil defence authorities issued alerts for Riyadh, Abha and Khamis Mushait before declaring the danger over. Reuters and Al Jazeera reported the coalition's statements. The Houthis had not immediately claimed responsibility for Tuesday's launches. Separately, the Houthi-run Saba agency reported Saudi strikes on Sanaa, a claim Saudi authorities had not confirmed at the time of reporting.

The Crow's analysis

The latest interceptions underline growing risks to Saudi cities and energy infrastructure amid renewed fighting in Yemen and a broader regional conflict involving Iran. Previous attacks damaged Aramco facilities and threatened oil infrastructure. The coalition's interception claims are not independently verified accounts of every military detail; the figures cited here come from Saudi officials. The economic consequences depend on whether attacks persist and on the security of pipelines and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb.

Counterpoint

There was no immediate Houthi claim of responsibility for these specific launches at the time of the reports. Accounts of strikes on Sanaa from Houthi-run authorities also require independent corroboration. There is no basis to report that the missiles struck central Riyadh or caused casualties in this incident. The accompanying city photograph was taken in 2022 and does not depict the attacks or interceptions.
