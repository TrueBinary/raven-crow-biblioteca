China extends suspension of rare-earth export controls until January

Beijing has prolonged by two months a pause in trade restrictions due to expire in November amid talks with the United States.

What happened

China's Ministry of Commerce announced on Monday, November 9, 2026, a two-month extension of its suspension of export-control measures covering rare earths and other strategic materials. Reuters and Folha de S.Paulo reported that the suspension will now run until January 10, 2027. The measures, announced in October 2025, cover selected minerals, rare-earth processing equipment and technology, as well as materials for lithium batteries and industrial diamonds. Some restrictions had previously been suspended following trade understandings between Beijing and Washington. The latest announcement came just before the previous pause was due to expire on November 10, 2026. It forms part of a broader extension of suspended trade measures between the two countries.

The Crow's analysis

The move lowers the immediate risk of renewed restrictions for manufacturers relying on Chinese critical-mineral supply chains, including electric-vehicle, electronics and industrial-equipment producers. It does not mean China has removed all export controls. The extension gives Beijing and Washington more time to negotiate while leaving uncertainty about what happens after January. China's central role in rare-earth processing remains a concern for governments and businesses seeking to diversify suppliers.

Counterpoint

The extension is not a final trade agreement and does not guarantee the controls will remain suspended after January 10, 2027. Reuters reported that the ministry's announcement offered no additional details. The accompanying historical satellite photograph shows a mine in China, not the November 9 policy announcement.
