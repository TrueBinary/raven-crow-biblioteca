Quaest poll39% expect Brazil's economy to worsen after Lula's reelection as optimism declines

Poll released on November 8 shows equal shares expecting improvement and deterioration, with public spending, debt and interest rates leading concerns.

What happened

A Quaest poll commissioned by O Globo and released on November 8, 2026, found that 39% of respondents expect Brazil's economy to improve over the next 12 months, while another 39% expect it to worsen. Nineteen percent expect no change and 3% did not answer. In August, before the presidential election, 46% expected improvement and 24% expected deterioration. Researchers interviewed 2,004 voters aged 16 or older in person from October 30 to November 2, with a two-percentage-point margin of error. Forty-eight percent said they were more worried about Brazil's future and 44% more optimistic. The economy, government spending, public debt and interest rates were the leading concern about the government for 27%.

The Crow's analysis

The shift points to weaker expectations for the coming year, but does not establish that the economy will enter a recession. The 39%-39% split contrasts with the 22-point lead held by optimists in August. Polling captures perceptions shaped by both economic conditions and political preferences. The findings follow Lula's remarks on interest rates and fiscal responsibility, which did not specify new measures. Inflation, employment, household income, credit and fiscal policy will offer more concrete evidence of economic performance.

Counterpoint

The same poll found that 39% anticipate improvement and 19% expect stability. Responses vary by income, region and political preference, and comparisons must account for sampling uncertainty. The poll cannot establish that reelection alone caused the change or predict future economic performance with certainty.
