White House says Trump plans nuclear-arms talks with Xi and Putin

US officials see the planned China meeting as a possible opening for negotiations, not an imminent deal.

What happened

On November 14, 2026, the White House said US President Donald Trump plans to discuss nuclear-arms reductions with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping at a meeting expected on Thursday on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen. Reuters and the Associated Press reported that US officials do not expect an immediate agreement and see the initiative as a possible starting point. It remains unclear whether Putin and Xi will join a specific three-way arms-control negotiation. The US-Russia New START treaty expired in 2026, while China has resisted joining nuclear limits talks with Washington and Moscow. The meeting is planned, not yet held.

The Crow's analysis

With no current bilateral US-Russia nuclear limits treaty, renewed dialogue matters. But the three countries' vastly different arsenals pose major challenges for verification, reciprocity and trust. Confirming the agenda and what is actually discussed will be crucial.

Counterpoint

A photograph of three leaders at a table could make history's front page; verifiably reducing warheads is much harder. The real test is whether concrete commitments and inspection mechanisms emerge.
