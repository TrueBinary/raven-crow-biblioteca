JUSTICE / Analysis and opinion

Ten arrested in Brazil as prosecutors investigate alleged R$ 5.5 billion Refit tax fraud

Prosecutors investigate suspected tax evasion and money laundering; Refit controller Ricardo Magro was not located.

What happened

On November 11, 2026, the Rio de Janeiro State Public Prosecutor's Office (MPRJ) launched an operation investigating alleged tax fraud and money laundering involving the Refinaria de Manguinhos (Refit) business group. Reuters and Agência Brasil reported at least ten arrests. Courts issued 17 arrest warrants and 19 search-and-seizure warrants in Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo. Investigators estimate R$ 5.5 billion in unpaid taxes and allege that distributors linked to the refinery were used to avoid tax payments throughout the fuel supply chain. Refit controller Ricardo Andrade Magro, described by prosecutors as the suspected leader, was sought but not located. The allegations remain subject to judicial scrutiny.

The Crow's analysis

The alleged use of an integrated corporate network to evade taxes could affect competition in fuel markets and state revenues. The R$ 5.5 billion figure is an investigative estimate, not funds recovered. Operations across two states show the inquiry's scope, but individual liability requires evidence and due process.

Counterpoint

Arrest warrants and detentions are not convictions. Refit disputes fraud allegations and is challenging tax debts in court. Reuters could not reach the company or Ricardo Magro's lawyers for comment on the day of the operation. Arrest counts are preliminary, and alleged organized-crime connections are not proven.
