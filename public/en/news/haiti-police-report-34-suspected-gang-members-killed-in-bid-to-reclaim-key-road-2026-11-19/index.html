HAITI POLICE REPORT 34 SUSPECTED GANG MEMBERS KILLED IN BID TO RECLAIM KEY ROAD

The Martissant operation targets a route linking Port-au-Prince to the south; authorities' casualty figures have not been independently verified.

What happened

On November 19, 2026, Haiti's National Police said at least 34 people it identified as suspected gang members had been killed in an operation in Martissant, south of Port-au-Prince. Police officers and a government task force took part. Authorities reported no police or civilian injuries, a claim that has not been independently verified. Reuters verified that police-released footage, including a building collapsing amid smoke, was filmed in Martissant. The offensive seeks to regain control of National Route 2, which links the capital with southern and western cities and has been disrupted by armed groups since 2021.

The Crow's analysis

Martissant's strategic importance lies in its location on the southern exit from Port-au-Prince. Armed groups have blocked traffic, demanded payments and attacked vehicles along this corridor since 2021, disrupting commerce, aid delivery and daily travel. Retaking territory could improve access, but a temporary military advance is not the same as reopening a road safely. The reported death toll alone cannot establish that the corridor is now secure or that residents can travel freely.

Counterpoint

The figure of 34 dead and the assertion that no civilians were injured come from Haiti's police, not an independent casualty assessment. Reuters and AP reported the use of explosive drones and a government task force supported by a private contractor. Analysts warned about civilian risks and the difficulty of holding territory after raids. Police said Salvadoran troops did not participate in this particular operation, although Salvadoran forces are active elsewhere in Haiti. The identities of all those killed and the lasting reopening of the route remain unverified.
