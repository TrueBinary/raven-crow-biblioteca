HARTHA POWER STATION FIRE IN IRAQ KILLS AT LEAST FOUR AND HALTS GENERATION

A fire at the Hartha power station near Basra spread to nearby homes, killed at least four people and halted operations.

What happened

A fire struck the Hartha power station north of Basra in southern Iraq on November 21, 2026, spread to neighboring homes and forced the plant to halt operations. According to Reuters, security officials reported at least 4 deaths and 35 injuries; Iraq's state-run Iraqi News Agency (INA) said civil defense teams brought the blaze under control. Reuters reported that the plant had 800 megawatts of installed capacity but was producing around 400 megawatts before the fire. Iraq's Electricity Ministry linked the incident to a gas pipeline supplying the facility, although the precise cause remains under investigation.

The Crow's analysis

The blaze was not confined to industrial infrastructure: it reached nearby homes, putting residents at risk. Halting a plant operating at about half its installed capacity removes active generation from an electricity network already struggling with maintenance and supply problems, according to Reuters. The plant's nominal 800-megawatt capacity must not be confused with its roughly 400-megawatt output immediately before the incident. The actual effect on electricity supply depends on the duration of the shutdown and whether other plants can compensate.

Counterpoint

Injury totals changed between early reports and later updates, and casualty figures may still be revised. An official reference to a gas pipeline is not a final forensic finding on the fire's origin or responsibility. The extent of the damage, the restart date and any resulting power outages remain unconfirmed.
