Storms affect 19,663 people across 33 municipalities in Brazil's Rio Grande do Sul

A new state Civil Defense report records 19,663 affected people, 60 injured and 50 without shelter after storms.

What happened

On November 21, 2026, the Rio Grande do Sul Civil Defense agency reported that recent storms affected 19,663 people across 33 municipalities in southern Brazil, according to a bulletin reported by Agência Brasil. The same agency reported 60 injured people and 50 people without shelter, with Tupanciretã among the hardest-hit cities. Agência Brasil reported three deaths in Tupanciretã, where damage included around 7,000 homes with roof damage and 20 damaged schools, figures subject to further field assessment.

The Crow's analysis

The statewide update shows that the disaster extends beyond Tupanciretã, where initial reports focused on casualties and destruction. On November 20, Civil Defense counted 32 incidents in 28 municipalities; the November 21 update identifies 33 affected municipalities. Incident counts and affected-municipality counts are different measures and should not be directly compared. The geographic spread increases demands on emergency shelter, power restoration, road access and municipal damage assessments.

Counterpoint

These figures remain preliminary and may change as field reports arrive. Housing damage estimates use different categories, including roof damage, structural damage and destruction, and cannot simply be added together. The precise meteorological classification of the winds in each municipality also requires technical assessment.
