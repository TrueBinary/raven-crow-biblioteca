SOUTH KOREA EDUCATION MINISTER RESIGNS OVER LAST-MINUTE AI ANTI-CHEATING RULES

Choi Kyo-jin resigned after last-minute changes to anti-cheating checks caused confusion ahead of South Korea's university entrance exam.

What happened

South Korean Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin resigned on November 22, 2026, in Seoul after criticism of last-minute changes to rules intended to prevent artificial intelligence (AI) cheating in the national university entrance exam. According to South Korea's Yonhap news agency, President Lee Jae Myung accepted his resignation that day after the government acknowledged confusion for test takers. According to Agence France-Presse reporting carried by the Guardian, officials identified 111 electronic-device-related misconduct cases during the November 19 exam, nearly half detected through emergency mobile-phone alerts.

The Crow's analysis

The episode highlights a practical challenge in supervising in-person exams: miniature cameras and phones can enable access to AI systems without an obvious computer. Authorities introduced checks of glasses, watches and clothing, device collection and restrictions on Wi-Fi around test venues. The measures changed shortly before the exam, after a candidate in a different examination admitted using a hidden camera and AI. The resignation is a political consequence of the planning and communication of the safeguards, not proof that the university exam was compromised on a large scale.

Counterpoint

The 111 reported incidents involved electronic devices and do not establish 111 confirmed cases of AI cheating. Officials said the exam ended without major incidents; widespread manipulation of results has not been demonstrated. No official video with verified content and a precise start time could be established for embedding.
