BRAZIL RECORDS NET FOREIGN EXCHANGE OUTFLOW OF US$2.301 BILLION IN NOVEMBER

The balance through November 19 was negative, but the 2026 year-to-date figure remains positive.

What happened

On November 25, 2026, in Brasília, the Central Bank of Brazil (BC) released preliminary figures for contracted foreign exchange transactions through November 19. According to the BC, November's balance was negative by US$2.301 billion. The institution's figures show a US$6.045 billion financial-channel deficit and a US$3.744 billion commercial-channel surplus. The year-to-date balance remained positive at US$26.421 billion.

The Crow's analysis

The difference between the two channels helps explain why trade receipts do not guarantee a positive foreign exchange balance. Financial transactions include investments, remittances and interest payments, whereas commercial transactions relate to exports and imports. Companies with foreign-currency obligations can monitor both channels to contextualize their exchange-rate exposure, without replacing an assessment of their own contracts.

Counterpoint

The figures are preliminary and cover only part of November. Contracted foreign exchange flows are not equivalent to changes in international reserves and do not alone determine the dollar exchange rate; nor can the entire financial outflow be attributed to a single cause.
