China's trade surplus tops $1 trillion through October as exports surge

Customs figures released Tuesday show October exports up 35.2%, raising trade tensions with the United States and European Union.

What happened

China recorded a cumulative merchandise trade surplus of US$1.04 trillion from January through October 2026, according to Chinese customs data released on November 10. It is the second consecutive year that the surplus has exceeded US$1 trillion, reaching that mark a month earlier than in 2025. Chinese exports rose 35.2% in US dollar terms in October compared with a year earlier, beating the 33.5% increase expected by economists polled by Reuters. Imports grew 41.6% over the same period. Despite the surge in overseas sales, the surplus for October alone narrowed to US$105.3 billion from US$133.5 billion in September as imports grew faster. Technology products, semiconductors and automobiles helped drive exports. Reuters and the Associated Press independently reported the figures using official Chinese customs data.

The Crow's analysis

The figures underline China's export strength and its reliance on foreign buyers to support activity amid weak domestic consumption and difficulties in the property sector. Global demand for artificial intelligence equipment has benefited electronics manufacturers. However, large trade surpluses heighten tensions with the United States and European Union, which are considering tariffs and restrictions on some Chinese goods. The cumulative trade surplus is not government fiscal profit: it is the difference between the value of merchandise exports and imports. October's sharp import growth also warrants caution when interpreting monthly changes.

Counterpoint

Chinese officials say they seek more balanced international trade rather than a deliberately widening surplus. Economists warn that reliance on exports and technology demand leaves growth vulnerable to trade restrictions or slower global investment in artificial intelligence.
