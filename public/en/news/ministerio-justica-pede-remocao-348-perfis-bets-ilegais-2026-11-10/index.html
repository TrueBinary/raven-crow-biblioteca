Brazil's Justice Ministry seeks removal of 348 influencer accounts promoting illegal betting

The government says the Instagram accounts have more than 150 million followers combined; requests for removal do not mean the accounts have already been taken down.

What happened

In a statement published on November 9, 2026, at 8:16 p.m., Brazil's Ministério da Justiça e Segurança Pública (MJSP, Ministry of Justice and Public Security) said it had requested the removal of 348 Instagram influencer accounts allegedly promoting illegal betting. The ministry said the accounts have a combined following of more than 150 million, including one account with 1.4 million followers. The operation involves the Secretaria Nacional de Direitos Digitais (Sedigi, National Secretariat for Digital Rights), the Secretaria Nacional de Segurança Pública (Senasp, National Secretariat for Public Security) and the Laboratório de Operações Cibernéticas (Ciberlab, Cyber Operations Laboratory). On November 9 alone, officials also identified 2,207 Facebook pages, 18 Telegram groups and 998 websites. Since September 25, monitoring has recorded more than 60,000 websites, pages, accounts, groups and servers associated with promoting irregular betting. The effort follows Provisional Measure No. 1,394/2026, which banned the operation, offering, intermediation and advertising of betting in Brazil. The ministry did not name the 348 accounts or confirm that platforms had already removed them all.

The Crow's analysis

The action shifts enforcement beyond betting websites to the influencer channels used to reach consumers. The figure of more than 150 million is a sum of account followers, not 150 million distinct individuals: one person may follow multiple accounts. The practical impact depends on how platforms respond and whether account owners challenge the requests.

Counterpoint

The official announcement reports monitoring figures and removal requests, not final court rulings against individual influencers. Claims that a platform is operating illegally must be assessed case by case, with transparent criteria and opportunities to challenge decisions. The statement does not justify accusing named individuals of crimes or saying that all 348 accounts have already been deleted.
