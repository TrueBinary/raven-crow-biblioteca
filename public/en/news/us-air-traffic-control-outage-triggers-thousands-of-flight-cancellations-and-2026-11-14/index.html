US air traffic control outage triggers thousands of flight cancellations and delays

After a nationwide halt to departures on Friday, airlines continue struggling on Saturday as officials investigate aging systems.

What happened

A communications and radar-data failure in the US air traffic control network prompted the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) to temporarily halt departures nationwide on Friday, November 13, 2026. The suspension lasted more than four hours and was lifted around 8:15 p.m. Eastern Time. According to the Associated Press, more than 2,000 flights were canceled during the shutdown and over 2,500 additional cancellations were reported on Saturday, November 14, as airlines worked to reposition aircraft and crews. These figures are time-specific snapshots and may change. Federal officials preliminarily linked the disruption to overloaded aging equipment during technology upgrades, but the precise cause remains under investigation. Officials said they had found no evidence of a cyberattack. Reuters independently reported the nationwide halt and gradual resumption.

The Crow's analysis

The disruption exposed the vulnerability of critical infrastructure and the difficulty of restoring airline schedules after a centralized outage. Even after departures resume, misplaced aircraft and crews can trigger cascading cancellations. Federal funding has been allocated to modernization, but the incident underscores the need for redundancy, testing and transparency about operational risks.

Counterpoint

When an air traffic network fails, restarting the system is not the same as restoring normal travel: passengers remain stranded while airlines rebuild schedules. The central question is why such vital infrastructure still relies on equipment that cannot absorb upgrades without disrupting service.
