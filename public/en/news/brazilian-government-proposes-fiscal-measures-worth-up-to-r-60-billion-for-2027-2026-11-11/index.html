Brazilian government proposes fiscal measures worth up to R$ 60 billion for 2027

Planning Minister Bruno Moretti outlines tax-break reductions and tighter spending controls; Congress has yet to approve the measures.

What happened

On November 11, 2026, Planning and Budget Minister Bruno Moretti said Brazil's government was preparing a supplementary bill expected to improve public finances by R$ 50 billion to R$ 60 billion in 2027. Speaking before Congress's Joint Budget Committee, Moretti said the proposal would authorize additional spending freezes to restrict real expenditure growth to 2% a year, compared with the current fiscal framework's 2.5% ceiling. The package also includes cuts to tax benefits, limits on tax-credit offsets and changes to how extraordinary oil revenue is counted for mandatory spending calculations. Folha de S.Paulo reported that Senator Jaques Wagner (PT-BA) would introduce the bill and Senator Eduardo Braga (MDB-AM) could serve as rapporteur. Reuters and UOL independently reported the announcement. The figures are government projections, not savings already achieved, and the bill has not yet been enacted.

The Crow's analysis

The package is intended to support the government's 2027 primary-surplus target of 0.5% of GDP amid debt and interest-rate pressures. An additional spending restraint estimated at R$ 12.8 billion and reduced tax breaks would combine expenditure control with potentially higher revenue. Excluding extraordinary oil receipts from the calculation base for mandatory expenditure could curb spending growth, including in healthcare, and requires scrutiny of its consequences. The outcome will depend on the final bill, congressional approval and actual budget execution.

Counterpoint

The projected R$ 50 billion to R$ 60 billion impact is a government estimate rather than audited savings. Congress may amend the measures, and businesses benefiting from tax incentives may oppose the cuts. Changes affecting mandatory spending, particularly healthcare, require social and legal assessment. Meeting fiscal targets will depend on implementation and actual revenues and expenditures.
