South Korea's top tax exam scorer admits cheating with generative AI

Candidate says he used a miniature camera, smartphone and AI in both rounds; police have opened an investigation.

What happened

On Sunday, November 15, 2026, the top scorer in South Korea's national certified tax accountant examination admitted using a miniature camera, smartphone and generative artificial intelligence to cheat in both rounds. He said he photographed questions and checked AI-generated answers, according to a video released through his lawyer. In April's first round he answered 159 of 160 questions correctly, scoring 397.5 out of 400, and also topped July's second round. Seoul Metropolitan Police confirmed receiving a written confession and opened an investigation. He also said he used the method in another professional examination.

The Crow's analysis

The confession highlights weaknesses in the supervision of in-person exams, even with proctors present. It comes ahead of South Korea's nationwide college entrance exam and has intensified calls for safeguards against concealed electronic devices.

Counterpoint

A confession does not replace a police and administrative investigation. Authorities must establish responsibility, review the evidence and determine any penalties under the law; this case alone does not establish widespread cheating.
