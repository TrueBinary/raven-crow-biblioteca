Merz presses EU for €45 billion for Ukraine; Slovak PM accuses him of blackmail

Germany links EU budget talks to a collective pledge to fund Kyiv, exposing divisions among member states.

What happened

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has urged all European Union member states to contribute to Ukraine's financing needs in 2027. In a letter to European Council President António Costa, he linked a collective commitment on support for Kyiv to discussions of other financial issues, including the EU's multiannual budget starting in 2028. Ukraine's projected 2027 financing gap is €70 billion, of which the EU is expected to cover around €45 billion. Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico accused Merz of 'blackmail' for tying budget talks to financial support for Ukraine. Slovakia, Hungary and the Czech Republic previously opted out of a €90 billion loan and have signaled resistance to another round. Reuters and The Guardian reported the dispute on November 10, 2026, while Euronews published details of the letter on November 9. No final agreement has been reached on the proposed aid.

The Crow's analysis

The clash pits Germany's push to share the cost of supporting Ukraine against governments wary of new financial obligations. As the EU's largest net budget contributor, Germany carries political weight, but other members' consent cannot be assumed. Linking Kyiv's military and budgetary support to the EU's 2028–2034 financial framework could increase pressure for a deal while complicating agreement on other priorities. The €70 billion figure is a projected 2027 funding gap, not an authorized disbursement.

Counterpoint

Merz argues that European security is indivisible and requires fair burden-sharing. Fico insists Slovakia has equal decision-making rights and rejects the linkage. Other governments warn that the strategy could obstruct both Ukraine aid and the wider EU budget. The proposed €45 billion contribution still requires negotiations and a concrete financing mechanism.
