BP offers Petrobras a stake in major oil discovery, but high CO₂ levels complicate a deal

Bloomberg and Reuters reported talks over the Bumerangue block; no agreement has been announced and technical risks remain under review.

What happened

BP has held talks with Petrobras about selling a stake in the Bumerangue block in Brazil's offshore Santos Basin, according to separate reports published by Bloomberg and Reuters on November 12, 2026. BP currently owns 100% of the block and has described its 2025 discovery as its largest in 25 years. Sources cited by Reuters say Petrobras technical teams are assessing the offer, with high carbon dioxide (CO₂) concentrations presenting a major obstacle. Bloomberg cited an estimate of roughly 8 billion barrels of liquids in place; that figure is not a proven commercially recoverable reserve or guaranteed production. No binding agreement has been announced, and the talks may not lead to a transaction.

The Crow's analysis

High CO₂ concentrations can substantially increase the cost of separating, processing and reinjecting gas from a reservoir. Petrobras is developing subsea separation technology, but Reuters' sources say its Jupiter field would take priority. Any investment in Bumerangue would require further geological and commercial evaluation, economic feasibility studies and decisions that have not yet been made. BP had previously said it would seek partners while appraising the discovery.

Counterpoint

Eight billion barrels sounds spectacular, but oil that cannot yet be produced profitably is a prospect, not cash flow. Before committing public-company capital, Petrobras should explain the cost of handling CO₂ and why this project deserves priority over existing developments.
