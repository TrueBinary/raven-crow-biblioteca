Congo Ebola outbreak tops 11,000 cases and 5,300 deaths, authorities report

The tally of 11,042 infections and 5,387 deaths makes it the country's deadliest outbreak; conflict is hampering medical care and surveillance.

What happened

The Democratic Republic of the Congo's Health Ministry reported on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, that the Ebola outbreak caused by Bundibugyo virus had reached 11,042 confirmed cases and 5,387 deaths. The figures were reported by the Associated Press and Reuters, as carried by Al Jazeera. Declared in mid-May, the outbreak has spread to eight of Congo's 26 provinces, with particularly difficult conditions in Ituri and North Kivu. It is the deadliest Ebola outbreak in the country's history and the world's second largest on record, after the 2014–2016 West African epidemic. Armed conflict, poor access to health services, delayed diagnosis and attacks on healthcare workers and facilities are complicating containment. Authorities are calling for safe access so medical teams can reach affected communities.

The Crow's analysis

The figures underscore the severity of the crisis: 5,387 deaths amount to almost half the confirmed cases, although that crude ratio is not necessarily an individual's risk of death after infection because diagnosis and reporting may be incomplete. The World Health Organization had documented the rapid expansion of Bundibugyo virus disease earlier in the outbreak and, according to November reporting, authorized trials of two candidate vaccines. No vaccine is approved specifically for this variant, making contact tracing, isolation, protection of healthcare workers and timely care crucial. Conflict and displacement complicate the response, without implying that neighboring countries face identical outbreaks.

Counterpoint

The figures are a snapshot that may be revised by health authorities. Confirmed cases may undercount infections in inaccessible areas, while the totals alone do not prove uncontrolled spread across all of Africa. Available sources do not establish that a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine is already widely available. The 2026 outbreak should not be confused with Congo's 2018–2020 Ebola epidemic.
