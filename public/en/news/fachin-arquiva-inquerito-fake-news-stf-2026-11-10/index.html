Brazil's Chief Justice Fachin closes seven-year 'fake news' inquiry

The head of Brazil's Supreme Court accepted the prosecutor-general's request to close Inquiry 4,781, while leaving earlier rulings and separate cases intact.

What happened

On November 9, 2026, Edson Fachin, president of the Supremo Tribunal Federal (STF, Brazil's Federal Supreme Court), ordered the closure of Inquiry 4,781, commonly known as the 'fake news' inquiry. The decision followed a request from the Procuradoria-Geral da República (PGR, Office of the Prosecutor-General), which argued that the investigation had fulfilled its purpose. The inquiry began in March 2019 under then-court president Dias Toffoli and was overseen for more than seven years by Justice Alexandre de Moraes. Fachin took charge in September 2026. He found that the original investigation could not remain open indefinitely as a vehicle for examining new allegations without a fresh assessment of its necessity. Agência Brasil reported that the closure does not invalidate investigative steps already taken, nullify existing criminal cases or terminate separate investigations still under way. Reuters independently reported the decision.

The Crow's analysis

The ruling draws a line under the original investigation at the center of a long-running institutional debate. Critics questioned its duration, secrecy and the concentration of powers within the court; supporters said it was necessary to address threats against justices and democratic institutions. Fachin's decision places a limit on the original inquiry while distinguishing it from other proceedings. Its consequences must be assessed case by case, rather than being treated as a blanket acquittal or annulment.

Counterpoint

The STF upheld the opening of the inquiry in 2020. Fachin acknowledged its institutional importance but concluded that this did not justify keeping it open indefinitely. Closing the case does not mean all previous decisions by Alexandre de Moraes have been ruled unlawful. The accompanying photograph dates from 2018 and does not show the November 9, 2026 decision.
