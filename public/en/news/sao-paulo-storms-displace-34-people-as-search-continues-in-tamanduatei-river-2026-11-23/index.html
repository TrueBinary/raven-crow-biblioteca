SÃO PAULO STORMS DISPLACE 34 PEOPLE AS SEARCH CONTINUES IN TAMANDUATEÍ RIVER

A collapsed wall in Penha displaced residents, while rescuers responded to flooding in Jabaquara and a missing person in the river.

What happened

Storms in São Paulo on November 22–23, 2026 caused a retaining wall to collapse in the eastern Penha district and prompted a search for a person swept into the Tamanduateí River near Vila Monumento, Agência Brasil reported, citing the state Civil Defense agency. According to Agência Brasil, 34 people were displaced after 2 homes on Rua Malacacheta were closed as a precaution: 14 residents stayed with relatives, while the owner of the second property pledged to relocate 20 tenants. The same report said 3 people, including a child, were rescued after floodwaters swept them away in Jabaquara and were taken for medical care. On November 23, Folha de S.Paulo reported a new severe weather warning at 4:18 p.m. and, citing utility Enel, 49,255 properties without power in the city at 4:47 p.m. Search results and incident totals may change with further official updates.

The Crow's analysis

The incidents highlight distinct risks: structural damage that requires professional inspection before residents return, and powerful urban flood currents capable of sweeping people away. The 34 displaced residents are not necessarily staying in public shelters. Renewed warnings on Monday afternoon show that the danger continued beyond Sunday's initial storm. Residents should avoid crossing floodwater and follow Civil Defense alerts.

Counterpoint

Available accounts disagree on the missing person's gender, so this report does not assert an identification. Totals may change as searches and inspections continue.
