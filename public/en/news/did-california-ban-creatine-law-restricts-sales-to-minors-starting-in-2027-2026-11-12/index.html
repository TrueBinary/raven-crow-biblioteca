DID CALIFORNIA BAN CREATINE?Law restricts sales to minors starting in 2027

The new rule names creatine as a factor when assessing covered products, but does not impose a blanket ban; it targets supplements marketed for muscle gain or weight loss and sold to minors.

What happened

California enacted Assembly Bill 2030, Chapter 720, on September 28, 2026; it takes effect on January 1, 2027. The law bars the sale, offer or delivery to people under 18 of certain dietary supplements marketed for muscle building or weight loss, along with nonprescription diet pills. Creatine is expressly named as one ingredient courts may consider when deciding whether a supplement is covered. This is not a statewide ban on creatine for all ages, and the presence of creatine alone does not automatically classify every product. Labels, marketing, displays and claims also matter. Retailers and online sellers must verify age for covered products; violations may carry civil penalties of up to US$ 500 each. The law includes an exception for minors with a prescription. News coverage on November 9 and 10 brought renewed attention to the rules for supplement stores.

The Crow's analysis

California's stated aim is to protect teenagers from products promoted for rapid weight loss or muscle growth, including supplements that may be mislabeled or contaminated. Industry groups argue that the absence of a definitive list of covered products creates uncertainty for retailers and consumers. Implementation will depend on how the statutory criteria are applied. Creatine monohydrate is an established nutritional ergogenic aid in sports science; the law does not declare it an anabolic steroid or an illegal substance. Its restriction concerns sales by age, not a universal ban on the compound.

Counterpoint

Imagine if this catches on: next, creatine could be treated in practice like high-risk or prohibited performance aids. The irony is that creatine already is a nutritional ergogenic aid, but that does not make it an anabolic steroid or a banned substance. Critics of AB 2030 question whether products with different risk profiles should face similar sales controls. Supporters argue the age restriction helps protect minors from misleadingly marketed weight-loss and muscle-building products. The law requires age checks; it does not make creatine illegal for adults.
