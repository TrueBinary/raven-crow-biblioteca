Brent oil closes at $95.82 as Saudi energy sites face attack claims and Hormuz tensions rise

Benchmark gains $3.06 on Thursday; Houthis claim strikes, but the extent of any damage remains unconfirmed.

What happened

Brent crude futures closed Thursday, November 12, 2026, at US$95.82 a barrel, up US$3.06 (3.3%). US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) settled at US$88.74, a rise of US$2.21 (2.6%). Prices climbed amid renewed concerns over Middle East oil supplies after Yemen's Houthis claimed attacks on a pumping station along Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, facilities in Yanbu and a Saudi Aramco building in Riyadh. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted two drones. The claims about targets hit and the extent of damage have not been independently confirmed. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz also faces disruptions and threats. Reuters reported an Iranian claim that four tankers were hit but could not verify it. Despite the day's rise, both benchmarks were down roughly 2.5% for the week.

The Crow's analysis

The rebound reflects renewed pricing of the risk to energy infrastructure and shipping routes, rather than necessarily indicating a confirmed loss of global production. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline and Yanbu terminal offer an alternative to the Strait of Hormuz. Higher prices could increase transport costs and inflationary pressure for importers, while inventories and diplomatic expectations continue to influence the market. The outlook depends on verified damage, export capacity and shipping security.

Counterpoint

Oil prices jump whenever conflict threatens more infrastructure. But a claim of an attack is not proof of damage. Treating every military statement as established fact would turn journalism into propaganda. Consumers may face higher costs long before peace arrives.
