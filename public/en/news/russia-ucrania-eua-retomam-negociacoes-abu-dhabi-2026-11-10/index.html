Russia and Ukraine resume US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi after nine months

Delegations discuss security guarantees, humanitarian issues and potential de-escalation, with territorial disputes unresolved.

What happened

Russian, Ukrainian and US delegations opened a new round of talks in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates, on November 10, 2026, the first US-mediated trilateral meeting since February. US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff are involved in the initiative. Ukraine's representative Rustem Umerov stressed security guarantees and a lasting peace, while Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky expressed hope for progress. The agenda covers humanitarian issues, including prisoners and detained civilians, as well as security and de-escalation. Reuters and the Associated Press reported the resumption of talks. No final agreement was announced, and discussions are expected to continue on November 11.

The Crow's analysis

The return to the negotiating table creates a significant diplomatic channel, but it does not mean a ceasefire has been agreed. The parties remain divided over Ukrainian territory, Europe's security architecture and Moscow's demands. European governments also seek a voice in decisions affecting the continent's security. Russian attacks on Ukraine's energy infrastructure continue as winter approaches. The crucial test is whether positive statements can become verifiable commitments while hostilities persist.

Counterpoint

Washington sees renewed negotiations as a chance to narrow differences. Kyiv insists on effective guarantees against renewed aggression, while Moscow ties a settlement to security and territorial demands Ukraine rejects.
