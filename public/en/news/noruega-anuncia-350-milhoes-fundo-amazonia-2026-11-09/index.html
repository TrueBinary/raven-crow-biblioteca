Norway announces R$ 350 million contribution to Brazil's Amazon Fund

Announced during COP31, the contribution recognizes lower deforestation rates and is tied to verified results.

What happened

Norway announced on Monday, November 9, 2026, a new contribution of approximately R$ 350 million, equivalent to 600 million Norwegian kroner, to Brazil's Amazon Fund. Norway's embassy in Brasília said the pledge recognizes Brazil's progress in reducing deforestation. Norway's Climate and Environment Minister Sigrun Aasland emphasized the forest's importance for the climate and biodiversity. According to Prodes/INPE (Brazil's satellite deforestation monitoring program) figures cited by the embassy, Amazon deforestation fell 41.8% in the 2024/2025 monitoring period to 3,335 km², while deforestation in the Cerrado declined 23.8% to 5,516.7 km². Norway said it has already contributed about R$ 3.8 billion to the fund since its creation. The announcement coincides with COP31 in Antalya, Turkey. The Amazon Fund is managed by BNDES (Brazil's National Bank for Economic and Social Development) and finances conservation, monitoring and sustainable activities.

The Crow's analysis

The pledge highlights international financing mechanisms that reward independently verified forest conservation results. The announced amount should not be mistaken for an immediate transfer: the contribution is linked to final verification of deforestation indicators, according to UOL. Falling deforestation is an important measure, but lasting results also depend on enforcement, land governance, community protections and transparency over spending. BNDES management and project evaluations provide a basis for monitoring the investment.

Counterpoint

Improved indicators do not mean deforestation, illegal mining or forest fires have ended. The announcement is a funding pledge rather than confirmation that the money has been disbursed. Payment depends on verification, while its actual impact requires tracking supported projects. The accompanying photograph dates from April 2011 and does not show the November 2026 announcement.
