Brazilian court temporarily blocks meal voucher rules for major operators

An injunction obtained by the ABBT trade group suspends fee caps, settlement deadlines and parts of the new payment-system requirements for member companies.

What happened

On November 9, 2026, a federal court in Brazil's Federal District granted an injunction requested by the Associação Brasileira das Empresas de Benefícios ao Trabalhador (ABBT, Brazilian Association of Employee Benefit Companies), suspending provisions of Decree 12,712/2025 for its member companies. Federal judge Frederico Botelho de Barros Viana of the 22nd Federal Civil Court issued the order, which covers operators including Alelo, Ticket, VR and Pluxee. The affected measures include a 3.6% cap on merchant fees, a 2% interchange-fee ceiling and a requirement to settle merchant payments within 15 days. The injunction also challenges requirements to open payment networks, although news outlets have described its precise implications for interoperability differently. The order is provisional and the federal government may appeal. Agência Brasil, Reuters and Folha de S.Paulo reported the ruling on November 9.

The Crow's analysis

The dispute pits the government's goal of increasing competition and expanding card acceptance against operators' argument that the decree exceeded the authority granted under Brazil's Programa de Alimentação do Trabalhador (PAT, Worker Food Program). Lower fees and faster settlement could reduce costs and improve cash flow for restaurants and supermarkets. The injunction temporarily limits the application of these obligations to ABBT members. Workers should not interpret the ruling as a cancellation of their meal voucher balances: the dispute concerns payment and acceptance rules.

Counterpoint

The government says the changes would increase competition and benefit merchants. The ABBT argues that the decree imposes requirements not provided for by statute and that technical adjustments are necessary. The injunction is not a final ruling on the decree's legality, does not automatically cover every operator and may be changed on appeal. Reports differ on the scope of the interoperability suspension; the full judicial order is authoritative. The photograph is archival.
