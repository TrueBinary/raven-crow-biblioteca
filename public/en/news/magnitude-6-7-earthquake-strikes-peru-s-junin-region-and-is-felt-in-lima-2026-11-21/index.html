MAGNITUDE 6.7 EARTHQUAKE STRIKES PERU'S JUNÍN REGION AND IS FELT IN LIMA

A magnitude 6.7 earthquake struck Junín, Peru, and was felt in Lima; no immediate casualties or major damage were reported.

What happened

A magnitude 6.7 earthquake struck the Junín region of central Peru on November 20, 2026, at 7:09 p.m. local time, according to Peru's Geophysical Institute (IGP). The IGP placed the epicenter 30 kilometers northwest of Junín and estimated a depth of 129 kilometers. The United States Geological Survey (USGS) also measured magnitude 6.7, but estimated a depth of 108 kilometers and located the epicenter 9 kilometers northeast of Carhuamayo. Reuters reported shaking in Lima, where residents evacuated buildings and lawmakers left Congress; no immediate casualties or significant damage were reported.

The Crow's analysis

The differing epicenter and depth estimates from IGP and USGS reflect calculations from different seismic networks, not two separate earthquakes. A deep focus helps explain why the shaking was felt far from the epicenter. Junín hosts mining operations, but Reuters did not confirm mine damage or shutdowns. Peru lies on the seismically active Pacific Ring of Fire. Residents should follow civil protection updates rather than unsupported earthquake predictions.

Counterpoint

Damage assessments were preliminary at the time of the Reuters report. IGP reported a magnitude of 6.7 and depth of 129 km, while USGS agreed on magnitude but calculated 108 km depth. There is no verified basis to claim deaths, widespread destruction or a tsunami. Seismic measurements and damage reports may be revised.
