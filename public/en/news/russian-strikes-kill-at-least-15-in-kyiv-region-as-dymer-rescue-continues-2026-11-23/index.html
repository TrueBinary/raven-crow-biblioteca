RUSSIAN STRIKES KILL AT LEAST 15 IN KYIV REGION AS DYMER RESCUE CONTINUES

Strikes hit Dymer, Vyshneve and Bila Tserkva; Ukrainian officials report at least 15 deaths.

What happened

Russian air strikes on towns in Ukraine's Kyiv region killed at least 15 people by November 23, 2026, according to Ukrainian officials cited by AFP and Euronews. In Dymer, about 45 kilometres north of the capital, a five-storey residential building was hit; the death toll reached 11, according to regional official Tymur Tkachenko as reported by Reuters on November 23. Euronews, citing local officials, reported three additional deaths at a supermarket in Vyshneve and one at a home in Bila Tserkva. Searches through the rubble continued and the casualty figures could change.

The Crow's analysis

The regional toll combines strikes in three locations rather than just the collapsed building in Dymer. This distinction matters: 11 deaths at the building and at least 15 across the region refer to different scopes of the same series of attacks. Searches for missing residents and further air alerts complicated rescue operations. The strikes come amid intensified Russian bombardment of Ukrainian towns and infrastructure ahead of winter, placing additional pressure on emergency services and civilians.

Counterpoint

These are preliminary figures attributed to Ukrainian authorities and may be revised. Reuters reported the official toll of 11 in Dymer; AFP, carried by Euronews, reported at least 15 across the region. The final casualty count and all military circumstances of the strikes have not been independently established.
