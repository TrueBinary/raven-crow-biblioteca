BRAZIL'S ECONOMIC ACTIVITY GAUGE FALLS 0.9% IN THIRD QUARTER, CENTRAL BANK SAYS

The IBC-Br declined 0.2% in September; agriculture, industry and services all contracted over the quarter, but the index is not official GDP.

What happened

On Monday, November 16, 2026, Brazil's Central Bank reported that its Economic Activity Index (IBC-Br) fell 0.9% in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the previous quarter, on a seasonally adjusted basis. Agriculture declined 2.0%, industry 1.1% and services 0.5% over the quarter. In September, the overall index fell 0.2% from August: agriculture rose 1.2%, while industry dropped 0.4% and services 0.1%. Compared with the third quarter of 2025, the index rose 0.7% without seasonal adjustment, and was up 1.4% over 12 months. The IBC-Br incorporates sectoral and tax data, but is not the official gross domestic product (GDP) figure, which is calculated by Brazil's statistics agency IBGE.

The Crow's analysis

The comparison periods matter. The 0.9% decline measures change between consecutive quarters, while the 0.7% increase compares the quarter with the same period a year earlier. All three sectors contracted quarter-on-quarter, pointing to a broad slowdown, but that does not establish that official GDP fell by the same amount. The more frequent IBC-Br release informs debate about growth and monetary policy; the Selic benchmark rate stands at 13.75% a year. Official GDP grew 0.5% in the second quarter, making the next IBGE release important for confirming the economy's direction. For households and companies, the report informs the discussion of credit and interest rates, but does not on its own prove an immediate change in jobs or income.

Counterpoint

A quarterly IBC-Br decline is not, by itself, an official recession finding or a definitive GDP forecast. The series can be revised and differs methodologically from IBGE's national accounts. A Reuters survey had forecast a 0.29% September decline, steeper than the rounded 0.2% result. Some reports provide more precise figures of about -0.25% for the month and -0.86% for the quarter; these are consistent with the rounded numbers used here. Official third-quarter GDP had not yet been released as of November 16, 2026.
