ASEAN SUMMIT KEEPS MYANMAR'S POLITICAL BAN, OPENS CONDITIONAL PATH TO RETURN

Leaders in Manila maintain non-political representation and demand sustained progress on their peace plan.

What happened

On November 15, 2026, leaders of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) formally decided in Manila to continue excluding Myanmar's political representatives from summits and foreign ministers' meetings. Only non-political delegates may attend until meaningful progress is made on the Five-Point Consensus, adopted after the 2021 military coup. The decision condemns attacks on civilians and civilian infrastructure and urges de-escalation. No ceasefire or readmission date was announced.

The Crow's analysis

Unlike the preparatory statements issued on November 14, this is a formal summit decision. ASEAN retained diplomatic pressure while explicitly allowing for a gradual restoration of full representation if progress is sustained and concrete. Its peace plan calls for an end to violence, dialogue, mediation and humanitarian aid. The bloc is balancing calls for engagement with the danger of normalizing military-backed leadership without improvements on the ground. Documented reductions in attacks and better aid access will be more meaningful tests than diplomatic visits.

Counterpoint

The leaders acknowledged insufficient implementation of the plan, despite limited progress on humanitarian assistance. Bilateral engagement does not mean automatic readmission or prove that peace has returned. No timetable was set.
