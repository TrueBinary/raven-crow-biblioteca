US records 5,412 measles cases in 2026, CDC says

The November 13 update shows 230 additional cases in one week; an official announcement on the country's measles-elimination status is still pending.

What happened

On November 13, 2026, the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) reported 5,412 confirmed measles cases through November 12, up 230 from the previous tally of 5,182. The CDC published the figures, which Reuters also reported. The total includes 5,394 cases reported by 47 jurisdictions and 18 among international visitors. The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) is reviewing whether at least 12 months of continuous transmission means the United States has lost its measles-elimination status. Reports on November 13 anticipated experts' conclusions, but no definitive public announcement by PAHO was independently verified during this reporting; this article therefore does not present the status change as officially announced.

The Crow's analysis

The increase underscores the importance of epidemiological surveillance and vaccination coverage. Elimination means an absence of sustained endemic transmission, not an absence of imported cases. Health authorities say two doses of the MMR vaccine provide strong protection and that immunization helps prevent outbreaks.

Counterpoint

Measles does not disappear because people choose to ignore it. Public debate should distinguish an officially announced health decision from media reports while recognizing the verified fact: cases are rising and prevention requires action.
