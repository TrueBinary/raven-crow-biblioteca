Brazil's central bank minutes flag fiscal risks as Selic stays at 13.75%

Minutes of the November 3–4 meeting highlight inflation expectations above target and the need for predictable public finances.

What happened

Minutes of the 282nd meeting of Brazil's Monetary Policy Committee (Copom), held on November 3–4 and released on November 10, 2026, detail the Central Bank's decision to hold the Selic policy rate at 13.75% a year. Reporting on the document says policymakers warned that fiscal uncertainty, inflation expectations above target and demand stimulus could hinder disinflation. The Central Bank's official rate history confirms that the 13.75% Selic target has been in effect since November 5. The minutes follow the November 4 announcement, when the committee paused after five consecutive rate cuts.

The Crow's analysis

The decision highlights the trade-off between controlling inflation and avoiding further pressure on lending and economic activity. Predictable fiscal policy can reduce risk premiums and support monetary policy effectiveness, while concerns over public debt may keep inflation expectations elevated. The minutes do not commit the bank to holding rates until a particular date; future decisions depend on inflation, growth and expectations.

Counterpoint

Businesses argue that high borrowing costs hinder investment and consumption. The Central Bank, however, is tasked with bringing inflation back to target and sees premature easing as a potential source of renewed price pressures. The minutes describe risks rather than a firm forecast of future rate changes.
