Taiwan reports unannounced Chinese naval drills involving more than 100 vessels

Taiwanese officials describe operations in the western Pacific involving the Liaoning carrier; Beijing calls the carrier's activities routine.

What happened

On November 13, 2026, Taiwanese officials said China had conducted large-scale, unannounced military drills in the western Pacific and near Taiwan earlier in the month. A Taiwanese security official told Reuters that more than 100 Chinese naval and coast guard vessels had been deployed at the operation's peak, allegedly rehearsing attacks and blockade scenarios. Those figures and scenarios remain attributed claims, not independently established facts. On November 10, Taiwan's Defense Minister Wellington Koo publicly confirmed that the Liaoning aircraft carrier group and other Chinese formations were conducting area-denial exercises in the western Pacific, as reported by Taiwan's CNA news agency. Beijing described the carrier's activity as routine training not directed at any country and did not confirm the details of the wider unannounced drills. The activity precedes the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

The Crow's analysis

The deployments highlight China's ability to concentrate maritime forces in a region central to Asian trade and security. Taiwanese officials interpret the drills as pressure designed to avoid the diplomatic fallout of a formally announced operation. Vessel counts and simulated scenarios should remain explicitly attributed until independently corroborated.

Counterpoint

If the goal is reassurance, assembling a war fleet near a neighbor is an unusual form of diplomacy. What matters is not only how many ships were at sea, but how to prevent shows of force from turning into a real confrontation.
