BNDES RELEASES R$2.664 BILLION IN CREDIT TO LATAM BRAZIL

The aviation fund loan carries an annual cost of 5.6% and seeks to ease fuel-related pressure on the airline's cash flow.

What happened

On November 26, 2026, the Brazilian National Bank for Economic and Social Development (BNDES) released R$2.664 billion in financing to LATAM Airlines' Brazilian operations. According to the bank's announcement, the funds come from the National Civil Aviation Fund (FNAC), linked to the Ministry of Ports and Airports, under a working capital facility for airlines operating domestic flights in Brazil. BNDES said the contract carries an annual cost of 5.6%, monthly principal repayments and a July 2031 maturity. The stated purpose is to ease operating cost pressures, particularly from aviation fuel. The bank also explained that Law No. 14,978 of September 18, 2024, authorized the fund's use to support airlines.

The Crow's analysis

Working capital financing provides resources to cover ongoing expenses and spread cash flow pressure over time. This differs from directly financing an aircraft purchase. The measure may help the airline maintain operating capacity as fuel costs rise, but its effects on routes, seat availability and fares will depend on commercial decisions. The use of a public fund also makes it important to monitor repayments, the fund's financial exposure and the results of aviation support policy.

Counterpoint

The disbursement is a loan, not a grant. The BNDES announcement does not establish that the financing will lead to lower fares or disclose the operation's net fiscal cost. LATAM Group posted net income of US$441 million in the third quarter of 2026, according to aviation publication Airway; this group-level result is not the standalone financial result of the Brazilian operation. Profitability does not eliminate cost risks, but underscores the importance of assessing the credit terms and public benefits.
