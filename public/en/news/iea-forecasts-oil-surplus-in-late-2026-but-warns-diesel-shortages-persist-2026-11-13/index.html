IEA forecasts oil surplus in late 2026 but warns diesel shortages persist

November 13 report points to recovering Gulf exports and supply exceeding demand, while shipping risks remain.

What happened

The International Energy Agency (IEA) said in its November 13, 2026 report that the global oil market is expected to return to surplus in the fourth quarter for the first time since the war in Iran began. Global supply increased by 2.3 million barrels per day in October to 105.4 million barrels per day. Gulf exports excluding Oman reached 18.4 million barrels per day, still 26% below pre-war levels. Observed inventories rose by 52 million barrels in October, their first increase since June. The IEA forecasts global demand falling by 2.7 million barrels per day on average in 2026, then recovering by 2 million barrels per day in 2027. These are forecasts, not guaranteed outcomes.

The Crow's analysis

Recovering Gulf shipments and weaker demand have eased crude oil shortages. The IEA reported that North Sea Dated crude fell from an early-October peak of $136 a barrel to about $93 when the report was prepared. But diesel supplies remain constrained, and attacks on vessels, energy infrastructure and maritime routes could reverse the improvement. The agency expects combined production in the United States, Canada, Brazil, Argentina and Guyana to rise by 1.6 million barrels per day in 2026. A crude surplus does not mean refined fuels will immediately become plentiful.

Counterpoint

More crude oil does not mean cheap diesel tomorrow. Refineries, freight costs and dangerous shipping routes still stand between the barrel and the fuel tank. The market recovery is a forecast, not proof that the crisis is over.
