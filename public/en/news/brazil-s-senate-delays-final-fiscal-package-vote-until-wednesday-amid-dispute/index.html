BRAZIL'S SENATE DELAYS FINAL FISCAL-PACKAGE VOTE UNTIL WEDNESDAY AMID DISPUTE OVER FOUR AMENDMENTS

The main bill has passed, but unresolved amendments prevent it from moving to the lower house.

What happened

On November 17, 2026, Senate President Davi Alcolumbre postponed consideration of four outstanding amendments to Complementary Bill 299/2026 until Wednesday, November 18. Rapporteur Eduardo Braga sought more time for negotiations. The Senate approved the core text on November 12 by 45 votes to 31. Disputed provisions include transfers to states and municipalities under the Kandir Law and a R$3 billion annual ceiling on federal pension compensation. The Senate has not completed its vote, so the bill has not yet moved to the Chamber of Deputies.

The Crow's analysis

Postponement does not overturn approval of the core text. Senators must settle the remaining provisions before sending the bill to the lower house. The government estimates the measures could improve the 2027 primary fiscal balance by up to R$56 billion. The primary balance excludes interest payments on public debt. This is a forecast, not realized savings. The proposal also changes spending-growth limits and tools for freezing expenditures under certain conditions. Amendments may affect the final rules and transfers to subnational governments.

Counterpoint

The delay reflects unresolved negotiations, not a final defeat. The Chamber of Deputies must still consider the legislation. Actual savings will depend on the enacted text and budget implementation.
