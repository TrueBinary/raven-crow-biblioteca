Corteva shares plunge as Brazil's farm-sector slowdown forces profit outlook cut

Crop-protection maker lowers its 2026 forecast as high borrowing costs and cash constraints weigh on Brazilian farmers' purchases.

What happened

Shares of US crop-protection company Corteva fell sharply on Thursday, November 12, 2026, after it lowered its full-year earnings outlook. The company said weakness in Brazil was a major factor: farmers face expensive financing, liquidity constraints and pricing pressure, while excess distributor inventory has delayed purchases. In results released on November 11, Corteva reported third-quarter global revenue of US$1.59 billion, down 9% year over year, and a pro forma loss of US$54 million. Organic sales in Latin America declined 31%. Its 2026 adjusted operating EBITDA forecast was lowered to US$1.1 billion–US$1.125 billion. Reuters reported that shares were down around 20% during Thursday's session; the market price fluctuated throughout the day.

The Crow's analysis

The results highlight how high interest rates, tight credit and excess inventory can ripple through Brazil's agricultural input supply chain. Weaker purchasing does not necessarily imply an equivalent drop in farm output, but it affects suppliers, distributors and investors. Corteva-specific factors, including the recent separation of its seeds business, also matter. Upcoming earnings will help show whether the weakness is temporary or extends into 2027.

Counterpoint

When borrowing costs rise and farmers postpone buying inputs, the impact can reach US stock markets. But attributing the entire share-price decline to one Brazilian policy would ignore inventories, competition, pricing and Corteva's own business decisions.
