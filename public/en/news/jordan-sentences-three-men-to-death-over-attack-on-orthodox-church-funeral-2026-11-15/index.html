JORDAN SENTENCES THREE MEN TO DEATH OVER ATTACK ON ORTHODOX CHURCH FUNERAL

The November 15 ruling concerns the October 23 attack in Karak that killed nine people; the sentence remains subject to judicial review.

What happened

On November 15, 2026, Jordan's State Security Court sentenced three men to death by hanging over the October 23 shooting at a funeral at St. George's Greek Orthodox Church in Hammoud village, Karak governorate. Nine people died. Reuters reports 34 injured, while other reports put the number at 25. Jordanian authorities said the suspects, two brothers and a relative by marriage, were arrested three days after the attack. The court convicted them of terrorism-related offenses resulting in deaths and weapons-related crimes. The ruling can be appealed and is automatically reviewed by the Court of Cassation.

The Crow's analysis

The judgment is a judicial response to violence against worshippers, but a death sentence is not an execution. The attack targeted a Christian minority community in Muslim-majority Jordan. Reuters also reports that Jordan later struck targets in southern Syria it linked to the attackers. Syrian authorities reported civilian deaths, prompting Jordan and Syria to agree on a joint committee. The case raises separate questions of criminal accountability, religious security and the consequences of cross-border military action.

Counterpoint

The reported number of wounded differs: Reuters and AP cite 34, while Al Jazeera cites 25. Judicial review is pending, and allegations against groups in Syria do not replace independent investigation of civilian casualties.
