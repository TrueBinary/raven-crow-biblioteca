DURIGAN SAYS BRAZIL IS PREPARING FURTHER FISCAL MEASURES BEYOND SENATE PACKAGE

Finance minister calls the bill under debate a first step and signals additional action, without detailing the proposals.

What happened

On November 18, 2026, Finance Minister Dario Durigan said at a Bradesco BBI event, following a meeting with President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, that the government intends to introduce further fiscal measures soon. He described the adjustment package under Senate consideration as only a first step, rather than the government's complete plan for the coming years. Durigan argued that stronger public finances would help create conditions for lower interest rates, ruled out capital controls and changes to the inflation target, and forecast slower growth in 2027 but not a recession. He did not release the full proposals or a firm legislative timetable.

The Crow's analysis

The new development is the promise of a second stage beyond the bill already before Congress. On November 11, the government presented measures it estimated could improve the 2027 fiscal balance by R$50 billion to R$60 billion; negotiations and amendments followed in the Senate. Announcing additional measures is not the same as passing them: the eventual effect will depend on the wording, negotiated exemptions, congressional approval and budget execution. The link between fiscal consolidation and lower benchmark interest rates also depends on inflation expectations and the independent decisions of Brazil's Central Bank.

Counterpoint

Durigan provided no detailed figures, specific spending cuts or explanation of which proposals might require constitutional changes. A 2027 primary surplus and lower rates remain government expectations, not guaranteed outcomes. Lawmakers are still negotiating the existing package, and further measures may face resistance. Without complete official texts, the additional fiscal impact cannot be quantified.
