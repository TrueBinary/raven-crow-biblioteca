Russia says it intercepted nearly 1,200 Ukrainian drones as strikes leave people dead

Large-scale attacks hit Russian regions; Ukraine reports a strike on a Volgograd refinery as Russian bombardments also cause casualties and blackouts.

What happened

On November 14, 2026, the Associated Press reported that Russia's Defense Ministry said nearly 1,200 Ukrainian drones had been launched at targets in Russia overnight. The figure is a Russian official claim, not an independently verified count. AP reported seven deaths and 17 injuries across three Russian regions, while Ukrainian authorities said they had struck a major oil refinery in Volgograd. Reuters separately reported four deaths and two injuries in a Volgograd industrial area, citing the regional governor, but could not independently confirm a hit on the refinery. In Ukraine, AP reported Russian attacks involving 117 drones, at least two deaths and power outages in 12 regions. These preliminary figures may change.

The Crow's analysis

The escalating drone campaign is putting energy infrastructure and civilians in both countries under growing pressure. Refineries are strategically important, but specific damage claims require independent evidence. The attacks come as US-led talks expand to European security arrangements, raising concerns among European governments.

Counterpoint

The drone war has become a contest of enormous numbers and official narratives. Nearly 1,200 is Moscow's stated figure, not an independently audited result. The immediate priority remains the deaths, injuries and disruption of essential services, regardless of which government releases the tally.
