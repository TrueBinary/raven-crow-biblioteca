Brazil's top court chief lifts secrecy on report mentioning Justice André Mendonça

The police report cites third-party conversations; a mention is not an allegation of wrongdoing

What happened

On November 20, 2026, Edson Fachin, president of Brazil's Supreme Federal Court (STF), ordered the release of a Federal Police (PF) report mentioning Justice André Mendonça in the Banco Master investigation. Mendonça himself requested disclosure and waived confidentiality. The report draws on messages recovered from Daniel Vorcaro, associated with the bank. News reports on Friday said police found 11 conversations in which third parties mentioned Mendonça, but no direct messages between him and Vorcaro.

The Crow's analysis

The police review sought to put references to STF justices in their proper context. According to reporting, Fachin forwarded four reports to the Prosecutor General's Office (PGR) and closed six others. Making the Mendonça report public enables scrutiny of the references, but does not mean investigators concluded that he committed a crime or disciplinary violation. Prosecutors must still assess any next steps. Being mentioned by others, communicating directly with a person under scrutiny and being formally investigated are different things.

Counterpoint

References in third-party messages do not, on their own, prove misconduct. The descriptions of the report come from news coverage; the full disclosed records and any subsequent prosecutorial findings may add context. Disclosure concerning Mendonça does not automatically release the reports concerning other justices.
