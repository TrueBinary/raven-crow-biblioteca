SAUDI-LED COALITION SAYS IT STRUCK 60 HOUTHI TARGETS IN YEMEN

The November 17 offensive escalates fighting near the Red Sea; Houthi officials report at least 17 deaths, a toll not independently verified.

What happened

On Tuesday, November 17, 2026, the Saudi-led coalition announced airstrikes in Houthi-controlled parts of Yemen, claiming it destroyed 60 military targets, including missile- and drone-related facilities. Houthi officials reported at least 17 deaths and additional injuries, saying strikes hit a sponge factory, a shoe factory and sites in the Saada area. The Associated Press reported the competing accounts. Neither the number of destroyed targets, the status of individual facilities nor the casualty figures had been independently confirmed.

The Crow's analysis

The offensive follows a withdrawal of Saudi-backed forces near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the strategic passage connecting the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden. This corridor is vital to shipping using the Suez Canal. The coalition also said it intercepted a Houthi cruise missile aimed at Riyadh. Beyond the reported target count, the combination of fighting and threats to maritime routes raises risks for Yemeni civilians and international shipping. The 60-target figure remains a coalition claim, not an independently audited tally.

Counterpoint

Houthi authorities report strikes on civilian facilities and fatalities, while the coalition describes military targets. Neither account alone establishes the legality of each strike or confirms the casualties. Independent access to affected sites remains crucial.
