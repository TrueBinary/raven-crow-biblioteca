BRAZIL'S CENTRAL BANK CHIEF CALLS FOR SPENDING-RULE CHANGES WITHOUT CUTTING SOCIAL PROGRAMS

Gabriel Galípolo says revenue-linked spending can fuel inflation and keep interest rates higher.

What happened

Gabriel Muricca Galípolo, president of the Central Bank of Brazil (BC), called for reviewing rules that automatically increase public spending without necessarily cutting social programs at the Itaú BBA Macro Vision 2026 event in São Paulo on November 23, 2026, Reuters and CNN Brasil reported. He argued that linking spending to revenue can add inflationary pressure when employment is high, complicating interest-rate cuts, according to Reuters. CNN Brasil reported that Galípolo ruled out any government discussion of changing Brazil's annual 3% inflation target, which has a tolerance band of 1.5 percentage points on either side. Reuters described the fiscal framework as limiting real spending growth to 70% of real revenue growth, with an annual ceiling of 2.5% and floor of 0.6%.

The Crow's analysis

The remarks do not amount to a new law or an interest-rate decision. They distinguish preserving social protection from changing mechanisms that automatically turn additional tax revenue into expenditure. If revenues rise but linked spending rises as well, the resulting fiscal adjustment may be smaller than expected. That is why the central bank chief connected spending-rule design with inflation expectations and government borrowing costs. Any legislative change would require political decisions outside the central bank.

Counterpoint

Galípolo offered no detailed legislative proposal and did not quantify how much inflation or interest rates might fall following changes to spending rules. The outcome would depend on the specific provisions, economic growth, revenues and safeguards for beneficiaries. His remarks express a risk assessment rather than a government policy decision.
