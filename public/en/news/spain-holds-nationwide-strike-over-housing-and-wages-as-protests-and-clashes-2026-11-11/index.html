Spain holds nationwide strike over housing and wages as protests and clashes erupt

UGT- and CCOO-backed walkout ahead of the November 29 snap election disrupts transport, ports and factories.

What happened

Spain is holding a 24-hour general strike on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, backed by trade unions and tenants' groups protesting rising rents, housing shortages and eroding purchasing power. Reuters and the Associated Press independently reported disruptions in transport, ports and automotive manufacturing, alongside demonstrations across the country. Isolated clashes with police and arrests were reported, including at picket lines in Madrid. UGT and CCOO said participation reached up to 90% at ports and in the automotive sector; those are organizers' figures, not independently verified national turnout. Spain is scheduled to hold a snap parliamentary election on November 29.

The Crow's analysis

The strike places housing affordability at the center of Spain's election campaign. Reuters cited a central-bank estimate of a roughly 750,000-home shortage. Unions want stronger tenant protections and higher wages, while employers advocate building more homes and cutting construction and purchase taxes. The scale of disruption varies by region and sector.

Counterpoint

Union turnout estimates should not be treated as a nationwide participation rate. Business lobby CEOE calls the strike counterproductive and argues that more housing supply and lower taxes would be more effective. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's government points to regional and municipal policies, but protesters also hold the national government responsible. Isolated clashes do not characterize all demonstrators.
