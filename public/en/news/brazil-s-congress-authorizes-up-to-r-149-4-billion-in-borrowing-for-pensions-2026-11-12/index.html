Brazil's Congress authorizes up to R$149.4 billion in borrowing for pensions and social benefits

Bill PLN 23/2026 won 402 votes in the lower house and 55 in the Senate; the spending was already budgeted and the bill awaits presidential approval.

What happened

On November 12, 2026, Brazil's National Congress approved a revised PLN 23/2026 bill allowing the federal government to borrow up to R$149.4 billion to finance spending already included in the 2026 budget. According to the Senate news agency, R$119 billion is allocated to pensions and other social security payments, R$26 billion to Bolsa Família and R$4.4 billion to the Continuous Cash Benefit (BPC) and Lifetime Monthly Income (RMV). The Chamber of Deputies voted 402 in favor, 12 against and one abstention; the Senate recorded 55 votes in favor and none against. The authorization creates an exception to the constitutional 'golden rule' limiting borrowing for current spending. It still requires presidential approval. The ceiling does not mean immediate bond issuance or new expenditure.

The Crow's analysis

The measure changes the funding source for existing mandatory expenditure rather than adding R$149.4 billion in primary spending. If bonds are issued, the government will incur debt-service obligations. The Joint Budget Committee recommended periodic reporting on borrowing, spending execution and the impact on public debt. The government says the measure protects benefit payments, while critics warn against recurring reliance on borrowing exceptions. Actual borrowing may be below the authorized ceiling.

Counterpoint

Congress did not find R$149 billion in a forgotten vault: it authorized borrowing to fund existing bills. Benefits must be paid, but transparency about bonds actually issued, interest costs and future fiscal sustainability is essential.
