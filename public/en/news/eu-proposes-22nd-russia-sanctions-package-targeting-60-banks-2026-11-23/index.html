EU PROPOSES 22ND RUSSIA SANCTIONS PACKAGE TARGETING 60 BANKS

European Commission proposes wider banking, tanker and sports restrictions; unanimous approval is still required.

What happened

The European Commission presented a proposal for the European Union's 22nd sanctions package against Russia in Strasbourg, France, on November 23, 2026, in response to the war in Ukraine, according to an official statement by Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. The Commission said the proposal would restrict transactions involving about 60 Russian banks and remove them from the SWIFT financial messaging network. It also proposed listing more than 70 additional vessels linked to Russia's shadow fleet, according to the Commission. Other proposed measures target energy services, cryptocurrency networks, visas for former Russian combatants and Russian-flag participation in European sporting events. The announcement did not bring the measures into force: approval by all 27 EU member states is required, the Associated Press reported.

The Crow's analysis

The proposal seeks to raise the financial and logistical costs of Russia's war effort through different channels. Bank restrictions would complicate cross-border payments; shipping and energy-service restrictions would affect export logistics; sports and visa measures have diplomatic and security aims rather than the same effect as a trade embargo. The previous, 21st package was adopted in July 2026, according to the Council of the European Union. A Commission proposal, unanimous political agreement and actual enforcement are separate stages.

Counterpoint

The announcement does not mean all 60 banks have already been disconnected from SWIFT or that more than 70 vessels are already subject to new restrictions. The measures may change during negotiations among the 27 governments. Their eventual economic or military impact cannot be quantified from the announcement alone.
