GLOBAL STOCKS EXTEND AI-RELATED SELLOFF; TAIWAN SHARES DROP 17% IN A WEEK

Technology shares fall worldwide as investors question AI spending and the outlook for US interest rates.

What happened

Stock markets in the United States, Europe and Asia extended their losses on Friday, November 20, 2026, amid selling concentrated in technology and artificial intelligence (AI) companies. According to a Reuters analysis published on November 20, global equities have lost nearly $9 trillion in market value since their August peak; Taiwan's stock index dropped around 17% this week, its worst weekly performance since 1990, Reuters separately reported. The Associated Press reported losses exceeding 5% in Japanese and South Korean stock benchmarks on Friday. Investors are questioning the sustainability of AI-related valuations and the financing costs of large data centers.

The Crow's analysis

The estimated $9 trillion decline is a change in the market value of outstanding shares, not an equivalent amount of cash withdrawn. Taiwan is especially exposed because of its semiconductor supply chain. High valuations, capital-intensive projects and expectations of elevated interest rates can pressure technology stocks and long-term investment at the same time. Reuters also reported that the VIX volatility index reached 30 while US government bonds attracted demand and gold and copper declined. A stronger dollar and reduced risk appetite may increase currency volatility in Brazil, but they do not alone determine the real's closing rate.

Counterpoint

Falling stock prices do not by themselves establish that the entire AI sector is a bubble or that its projects will fail. Intraday moves can change before markets close, while economic consequences depend on profits, interest rates and capital flows. The global loss estimate measures market capitalization rather than realized losses for every investor.
