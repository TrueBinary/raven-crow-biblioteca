Trump says he pressed the Philippines to replace Brazilian farm imports with US products

Trump targets Brazilian farm suppliers, while Manila discusses US soybeans and wheat without confirming a ban on Brazilian imports.

What happened

On November 20, 2026, US President Donald Trump said at a White House news conference in Washington that he had pressured the Philippines to replace agricultural goods from countries including Brazil with American products, according to the Associated Press (AP) and CNN Brasil. Trump claimed the Philippines would buy 100% of its agricultural goods from the United States, a figure reported as his assertion by AP but not confirmed in the published agreement terms. The Philippine News Agency (PNA) reported on the same date that Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. discussed increasing purchases of US soybeans and wheat, without announcing an end to Brazilian imports.

The Crow's analysis

Trump's remarks could sharpen competition between Brazilian and American farm exporters, but they do not establish that existing contracts were cancelled or that Brazilian soybean shipments have been prohibited. In a November 18, 2026 statement, the White House said the two governments aimed to narrow the US trade deficit with the Philippines through more American agricultural and energy exports, without specifying a 100% exclusive purchasing requirement. The White House also described more than US$17 billion in commercial deals and investments across multiple sectors; that total is not a soybean purchase commitment. Any effect on Brazilian exporters depends on actual contracted quantities, schedules and supplier substitution, none of which has been established.

Counterpoint

The Philippine News Agency quoted Marcos Jr. describing the talks as a way to diversify food and energy supplies and saying details remain under negotiation. AP noted that the White House materials did not include Trump's claimed percentage. Soybeans featured in Manila's discussions of additional US purchases, not in a verified decision to ban Brazilian soybeans. The colloquial reaction in the Portuguese headline is editorial language, not a verified economic outcome.
