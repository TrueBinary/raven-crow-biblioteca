ANGLO AMERICAN WARNS BRAZILIAN NICKEL MINES COULD CLOSE IF EU BLOCKS SALE

The company told European customers it would seek to close Barro Alto and Codemin if the proposed sale to MMG is rejected.

What happened

On November 17, 2026, Anglo American warned European ferronickel customers that it intends to place its Barro Alto and Codemin operations in Goiás, Brazil, into care and maintenance with a view to closure if the European Commission blocks their sale to MMG. Reuters reported the warning after obtaining a letter from chief executive Duncan Wanblad. The transaction is valued at up to $500 million. This is a conditional warning, not an immediate closure announcement or a final regulatory veto. The Commission is due to decide by December 21.

The Crow's analysis

Ferronickel is mainly used to manufacture stainless steel. This explains why a sale involving Brazilian assets faces European competition scrutiny: regulators are assessing potential effects on suppliers and buyers in the European Union. Hong Kong-listed MMG, majority-owned by China Minmetals, argues that it would maintain supply and invest in the business. In September it acknowledged receiving a formal statement of objections, a preliminary step rather than a prohibition. A November 3 filing extended the contractual completion deadline to December 31. The dispute pits the regulator's supply-diversion concerns against the companies' warning of possible production shutdowns.

Counterpoint

Anglo American's warning describes what the company says it would do if the deal fails; it does not establish that the mines will close. The European Commission has not issued its final decision, and MMG disputes its competition concerns. Reuters reported that revised proposed commitments would increase annual European ferronickel supply from 11,500 to 16,000 metric tons, but their acceptance has not been confirmed. Actual job losses cannot yet be quantified.
