Brazilian real weakens as dollar closes at R$5.868, highest in 19 months

The dollar rises for a fifth consecutive session while the Ibovespa falls 1.34%.

What happened

On November 18, 2026, the US dollar closed at R$5.868 in Brazil, up 1.48% on the day and its highest closing level since April 15, 2025. It was the fifth consecutive daily increase. The Ibovespa fell 1.34% to 167,537.95 points. Brazil's Central Bank held two foreign-exchange liquidity auctions totaling US$1 billion with future repurchase commitments. Agência Brasil cited both global dollar strength and uncertainty over Brazil's fiscal package.

The Crow's analysis

Domestic fiscal concerns and international interest-rate expectations both matter. Agência Brasil reported a 0.23% increase in the global dollar index, compared with a 1.48% rise in Brazil's exchange rate, illustrating why the move cannot simply be attributed to global markets. A weaker real can increase costs for importers and companies with dollar-denominated liabilities, although the effect on consumer prices depends on other conditions.

Counterpoint

One trading session cannot establish a single cause or predict the next move. The central bank's liquidity auctions do not imply a target exchange rate. These are November 18 closing figures, not live quotes.
