Researchers report theft of more than 2,000 Tajik government documents with AI assistance

CloudSEK links the operation to Silent Lynx; indications of a Kazakhstan connection do not establish state responsibility.

What happened

Cybersecurity company CloudSEK published findings on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, alleging that operators associated with the Silent Lynx group obtained more than 2,000 Tajikistan national-security-related documents. The company said researchers found exposed attacker infrastructure containing records of the Hermes artificial intelligence agent. Reuters reported that the agent allegedly assisted with reconnaissance, coding and organizing files taken from a government electronic document system. CloudSEK also described access to Tajikistan's railway network and an Uzbek geospatial platform. These are findings from a private investigation, without independent public confirmation from the governments concerned.

The Crow's analysis

The case suggests that AI agents able to carry out multistep tasks may accelerate cyberespionage, particularly where attackers already possess compromised credentials and exploit weak access controls. The report describes a human operator directing the agent, not an AI independently initiating the operation. CloudSEK said reconnaissance began in August 2026, with operational records extending into October. The full scope and origin of the intrusions require further investigation.

Counterpoint

CloudSEK expressed high confidence in linking the exposed infrastructure to Silent Lynx, but only medium confidence in a possible Kazakhstan nexus based on language, working hours and internet connections. These clues do not prove involvement by the Kazakh government. Reuters said Kazakh and Tajik officials had not immediately responded to requests for comment. The company's technical account has not been independently verified in full.
