NASA CONFIRMS VOYAGER 1 IS ONE LIGHT-DAY FROM EARTH AS DATA TROUBLE PERSISTS

Launched in 1977, the probe has passed 25.9 billion kilometers; its radio signals now take 24 hours to reach Earth.

What happened

The United States National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) confirmed on November 19, 2026, that Voyager 1 reached a distance of one light-day from Earth in interstellar space on November 18, approximately 25.9 billion kilometers. According to NASA, the spacecraft's radio signal takes about 24 hours to reach the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) in California. The mission launched in 1977 and entered interstellar space in 2012; NASA says it has not received science and engineering data since November 14, 2026, although the radio carrier signal remains detectable.

The Crow's analysis

A light-day measures distance, not travel time: it is how far light moves through a vacuum in 24 hours. For mission controllers, sending a command and receiving a reply now involves about 48 hours of signal travel, excluding processing time. Voyager 1 did not travel this distance in a day; the journey took almost 49 years. The milestone demonstrates long-distance communications beyond the heliosphere, the region shaped by the solar wind. Its radioisotope power supply loses about four watts annually, forcing engineers to conserve energy.

Counterpoint

NASA has confirmed the distance milestone, not the full restoration of spacecraft data. Detecting a carrier signal is not equivalent to receiving usable science and engineering information. The cause of the outage and the timetable for a fix remain uncertain. Current close-up photographs of Voyager 1 in interstellar space do not exist; published depictions are illustrations or archival images.
