White House confirms Trump, Xi and Putin meeting in China for nuclear arms talks

The three leaders are due to meet on November 19 at the APEC summit in Shenzhen; US officials do not expect an immediate deal.

What happened

On Friday, November 13, 2026, the White House confirmed plans for Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin to meet on Thursday, November 19, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, China. Spokesperson Olivia Wales said Trump intends to discuss reducing nuclear arsenals. A US official cautioned that Washington does not expect an agreement at the meeting. Reuters and the Associated Press reported the announcement. The meeting has not yet taken place and the schedule may change.

The Crow's analysis

The talks would bring together leaders of three nuclear powers amid tensions over Ukraine and Iran. The expiration of the US-Russia New START treaty in February 2026 has increased pressure for new arms-control arrangements. China has been reluctant to join a framework modeled on the far larger US and Russian arsenals. Announcing talks is not the same as reaching an agreement.

Counterpoint

A photograph of three leaders at a table does not dismantle a single warhead. The real test is whether the talks produce verifiable commitments rather than statements of intent. Any discussion of Ukraine must also take Ukrainians' position into account.
