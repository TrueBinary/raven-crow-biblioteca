ADNOC REPORTS 11 INJURED AND ONE MISSING AFTER VESSEL ATTACK IN HORMUZ

The UAE company confirmed Wednesday that its vessel was attacked Tuesday; the search continues and responsibility remains unestablished.

What happened

On November 18, 2026, UAE-based ADNOC Logistics & Services said one of its vessels was attacked on Tuesday, November 17, in the Strait of Hormuz. The company reported 11 injured crew members and one person still missing; the others were evacuated. ADNOC did not name the vessel in the statement reported by Reuters. Omani authorities separately reported rescuing crew from the Panama-flagged LPG tanker Tara Gas, but the two accounts should not be treated as referring to the same vessel without confirmation. The attack's perpetrator and exact circumstances have not been publicly established.

The Crow's analysis

The Strait of Hormuz links the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and is a strategic energy shipping corridor. Beyond the casualty figures, the missing seafarer and evacuation underscore the immediate risks to civilian crews. Repeated incidents also add uncertainty to insurance, shipping routes and oil and gas transport. Reports about separate ships should not be combined into a single casualty count. Distinguishing company statements, confirmed rescue operations and military claims is crucial.

Counterpoint

ADNOC did not initially identify the vessel or formally assign responsibility. Maritime accounts of a projectile and Iranian military claims about other vessels do not establish who carried out this attack. Casualty figures and rescue operations may change with verified updates.
