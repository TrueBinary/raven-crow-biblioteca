BRAZIL EASES BUDGET SPENDING RESTRICTIONS BY R$3.4 BILLION

The revision leaves R$12.6 billion in spending restrictions and incorporates a fiscal exception for diesel subsidies.

What happened

In Brasília on November 25, 2026, Brazil's federal government presented its bimonthly budget review and announced reduced spending restrictions. According to the Ministry of Planning and Budget (MPO), the restricted amount fell by R$3.4 billion to R$12.6 billion. The ministry said R$12.3 billion remains subject to fiscal-target restrictions and R$300 million to spending-cap freezes. Complementary Law 239/2026, signed on November 24, allows up to R$10.5 billion in diesel subsidies to be excluded from the fiscal target calculation, according to the MPO.

The Crow's analysis

Spending-cap freezes enforce expenditure limits, while fiscal-target restrictions align budget execution with the primary balance target. Easing restrictions increases room for authorized spending but does not mean all funds will be spent immediately. The legal exclusion of diesel subsidies changes the target calculation without eliminating actual Treasury payments.

Counterpoint

Minister Bruno Moretti argues that fiscal consolidation remains on track. However, Agência Brasil reports that the projected actual primary deficit increased from R$67.26 billion to R$76.98 billion. The final outcome depends on revenue and spending through December.
