CEARÁ ELECTORAL COURT MAJORITY BACKS CIRO GOMES CONVICTION, BUT CASE REMAINS OPEN

Four of seven judges voted to reject the former governor's appeal in a political gender-violence case involving Janaína Farias.

What happened

On Monday, November 16, 2026, four of the seven members of Ceará's Regional Electoral Court (TRE-CE) voted to uphold a lower-court conviction of former governor Ciro Gomes (PSDB) for political gender-based violence against Janaína Farias (PT), now mayor of Crateús. The appeal hearing was suspended after judge Antônio Edilberto Oliveira Lima requested more time to review the case; no resumption date was announced. Rapporteur Arsênia Parente Breckenfeld and Luciana Melo Madruga Fernandes Arruda had voted on November 9. Luiz Evaldo Gonçalves Leite and José Maximiliano Machado Cavalcanti joined them on November 16. The case concerns remarks made in 2024, when Farias was serving as a senator. The initial sentence was one year and four months of imprisonment, replaced with alternative measures. The appellate judgment has not been completed.

The Crow's analysis

A majority vote is not the same as a completed judgment. Although the current tally is four to zero, judges may change their votes before the decision is formally announced. Electoral disqualification depends on the final outcome and the requirements of Complementary Law 64/1990, Brazil's Clean Record Law. Article 326-B of the Electoral Code, introduced by Law 14,192/2021, addresses gender-based political violence aimed at obstructing women's political participation or exercise of office. The court must distinguish robust political criticism from statements that, according to prosecutors, demean a woman through sexual or intimate references. The next verifiable development is the resumed hearing and formal decision.

Counterpoint

Gomes appealed the lower-court ruling. UOL reported that his defense argued the remarks targeted other people, while the trial court found that they went beyond political criticism. No new defense statement after the partial vote was confirmed. Reports cite different financial figures because alternative penalties and criminal fines are separate matters; without the full appellate decision, no amount should be presented as final. Further appeals may remain possible.
