Study flags environmental and social sensitivities at 89.4% of mapped Brazilian wind farms

Farol das Eólicas maps sensitive areas; alerts are not proof of environmental damage

What happened

A survey by the International Arayara Institute, released on November 19, 2026 at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, mapped 1,748 onshore wind projects across 13 Brazilian states, including operating plants and projects under construction or planning. According to the Farol das Eólicas platform, 89.4% have at least one environmental or social sensitivity factor. The survey placed 645 projects in the red category (36.9%), 917 in yellow (52.5%), and 186 in green (10.6%). Agência Brasil reported the findings on November 20.

The Crow's analysis

An alert is not the same as verified harm. The tool overlays project locations with traditional communities' territories, conservation areas, biodiversity sites and nearby homes, using 13 criteria. The results may help prioritize licensing reviews and site selection, but do not quantify actual harm or replace project-specific environmental assessments. Of the 1,748 projects surveyed, 1,175 are operating, so the overall percentage does not describe operating facilities alone. The institute also identified at least one home within 400 meters of 64.5% of the sites.

Counterpoint

The sensitivity scores do not prove that 89.4% of wind farms caused damage or breached permits. The institute itself says its platform cannot replace environmental impact studies. The cited report does not provide responses from individual operators. These are findings from a civil-society survey, not conclusive enforcement findings.
