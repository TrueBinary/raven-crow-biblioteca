Russia and Ukraine trade strikes on capitals as US envoys arrive in Moscow

Following Abu Dhabi talks, explosions were reported in Kyiv and drones were intercepted near Moscow as Washington seeks renewed diplomacy.

What happened

Russia and Ukraine reported renewed attacks early on Thursday, November 12, 2026, hours before US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Moscow for talks with Vladimir Putin. Explosions were heard in Kyiv, according to media reports, while Russia's Defence Ministry said it had struck Ukrainian military and energy facilities. Ukrainian officials reported damage to civilian and energy infrastructure in the Rivne region. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said five drones heading toward the Russian capital had been shot down; these official figures have not been independently verified. Two days of negotiations in Abu Dhabi ended without a ceasefire but with an agreement to exchange prisoners, according to Reuters and UOL reports.

The Crow's analysis

The renewed attacks underscore the gap between diplomacy and the battlefield. Territory and security guarantees remain major obstacles to a broader agreement. The US envoys are expected to meet Putin before travelling to Ireland for talks involving Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and European leaders. Progress on a prisoner exchange is meaningful but does not amount to a peace settlement. Claims about damage and interceptions are attributed to the relevant authorities rather than presented as independently established facts.

Counterpoint

If sitting around a negotiating table were enough to silence weapons, this war would already be over. Diplomats discuss borders while civilians remain exposed to strikes and power disruptions. A prisoner exchange can ease immediate suffering, but peace requires verifiable commitments, not just photographs of meetings. Official claims must also be distinguished from independently verified damage.
