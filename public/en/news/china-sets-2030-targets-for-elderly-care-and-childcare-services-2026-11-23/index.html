CHINA SETS 2030 TARGETS FOR ELDERLY CARE AND CHILDCARE SERVICES

China sets targets for nursing beds, community elderly care and childcare enrolment by 2030.

What happened

China's National Development and Reform Commission published a national plan on November 23, 2026 to expand elderly care and childcare services by 2030 amid population ageing and declining births. According to the official plan, nursing-care beds are to account for 73% of beds in elderly care institutions by 2030, while community elderly care facilities are to reach 70% coverage. The commission also targets a 6-percentage-point increase in childcare enrolment for children under three compared with 2025. These are targets, not evidence that the services have already expanded to those levels.

The Crow's analysis

The plan addresses two demographic pressures at once: rising demand for elderly assistance and falling births. It calls for upgrading institutions, improving neighbourhood services and encouraging workplace childcare, backed by central funding, local-government bonds and commercial credit. The 73% figure measures the share of nursing beds inside institutions, not the share of older people receiving care; the 70% figure concerns facility coverage, not guaranteed access for every resident.

Counterpoint

The 2030 figures are policy targets rather than achieved outcomes. Implementation depends on staffing, funding and local administrative capacity; the plan does not establish equal access in urban and rural areas.
