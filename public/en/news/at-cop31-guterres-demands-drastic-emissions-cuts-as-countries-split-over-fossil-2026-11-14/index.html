At COP31, Guterres demands drastic emissions cuts as countries split over fossil fuels

The UN chief urged immediate reductions in Türkiye while negotiators disagreed over explicit targets for oil, gas and coal.

What happened

On November 13, 2026, at COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye, UN Secretary-General António Guterres called for immediate and drastic reductions in greenhouse gas emissions. Reuters and the Associated Press reported his appeal and divisions over how the final negotiating text should address fossil fuels. The summit runs from November 9 to 20. Countries are discussing efforts to expand electrification, but the proposed increase in electricity's share of global energy consumption does not require all power to come from renewable sources. No final agreement was announced that day.

The Crow's analysis

Negotiations pit climate urgency against transition costs, energy security and the financing needs of developing economies. Electrification alone cannot guarantee matching emissions cuts if electricity continues to be generated from high-emission sources. Funding and measurable implementation remain essential.

Counterpoint

Strong speeches are easier than cutting emissions without increasing costs for households. COP31 needs workable targets, transparent results and real financing, not merely another declaration.
