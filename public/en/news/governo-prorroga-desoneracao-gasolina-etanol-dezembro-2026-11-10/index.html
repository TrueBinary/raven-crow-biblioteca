Brazil extends gasoline and ethanol tax relief through December 9, with estimated cost above R$ 3.5 billion

Decree 13,159 keeps federal fuel taxes at zero and extends an ethanol subsidy for another 30 days amid higher global oil prices.

What happened

Brazil extended federal tax relief on gasoline and hydrous ethanol through December 9, 2026. Decree 13,159 was signed on November 9 by acting President Geraldo Alckmin and published in an extraordinary edition of the Official Gazette. PIS/Pasep, Cofins and Cide fuel taxes on gasoline remain at zero, excluding aviation gasoline; PIS/Pasep and Cofins on hydrous ethanol also remain at zero. According to the Finance Ministry, the relief equals R$ 0.89 per liter of gasoline A and R$ 0.19 per liter of ethanol. A subsidy of R$ 0.428 per liter of hydrous ethanol paid to producers and cooperatives was also extended. The ministry estimates a R$ 2.76 billion fiscal impact from tax relief and R$ 813.41 million from the ethanol subsidy over 30 days.

The Crow's analysis

The extension prevents the benefits from expiring immediately amid higher international oil prices linked to the Middle East conflict. The Finance Ministry says it will offset the R$ 2.76 billion tax relief through extraordinary government revenues from the oil sector. The policy may cushion fuel prices but does not determine retail pump prices, which also depend on blending, refining, distribution, commercial margins and state taxes. Repeated extensions warrant scrutiny of fiscal costs and how long external shocks persist.

Counterpoint

The government argues that the temporary measure protects consumers and preserves ethanol competitiveness. Critics may question the sustainability of recurring tax relief and subsidies and how much of the benefit reaches motorists. Announced amounts are fiscal estimates, not evidence of equivalent retail price reductions.
