Fux moves Brazil's 6x1 workweek ruling to Supreme Court's in-person plenary

The procedural request restarts voting in person; Justice Flávio Dino's interim transition order remains in effect.

What happened

On November 14, 2026, Brazilian Supreme Federal Court (STF) Justice Luiz Fux requested that the virtual review of the transition away from the six-days-on, one-day-off work schedule be moved to an in-person plenary session. Court president Edson Fachin will set a new date. Four justices had supported rapporteur Flávio Dino's interim order before the interruption: Dino, Alexandre de Moraes, Cármen Lúcia and Gilmar Mendes. Voting will restart in person, but Dino's interim order remains in force. The transition is scheduled to begin on December 16, 2026, reducing weekly hours from 44 to 42, with a further reduction to 40 hours in December 2027.

The Crow's analysis

The procedural move does not repeal the workweek reform or automatically halt its implementation. Business groups are challenging the constitutionality and adjustment deadlines, while collective bargaining and rest-day arrangements remain key questions.

Counterpoint

The court must balance legal certainty, employers' transition costs and workers' rest. Moving a case to the plenary is not the same as striking down the reform: the interim order still applies.
