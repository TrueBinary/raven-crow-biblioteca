BRAZIL / Analysis and opinion

Storms affect 25,123 people in 42 municipalities in southern Brazil; death toll reaches five

Rio Grande do Sul Civil Defense updates its toll to 25,123 affected people and incidents in 42 municipalities.

What happened

On November 22, 2026, the Rio Grande do Sul Civil Defense agency reported that storms beginning on November 20 had affected 25,123 people in 42 municipalities in southern Brazil, according to Agência Brasil. The agency's figures, reported by Agência Brasil, include 47 incidents, 265 injured people and five deaths. The assessment also lists 19,854 displaced from their homes, 40 people requiring public shelter and 7,971 homes with roof damage; local damage assessments remain subject to revision.

The Crow's analysis

The new figures expand the scale of the emergency compared with the November 21 report of 19,663 affected people across 33 municipalities: an increase of 5,460 people and nine municipalities, although the underlying records may be revised. The rise in reported injuries from 60 to 265 underscores the distinction between newly recorded cases and injuries necessarily sustained during the latest reporting interval. Damage to 29 educational facilities and three healthcare facilities also poses challenges for public services alongside housing repairs and shelter.

Counterpoint

The figures are preliminary and depend on municipal reporting. People displaced from their homes and those requiring public shelter are distinct categories. Differences between reports do not establish that every newly recorded case occurred between publication times.
