Dollar closes at R$5.65, highest since June 2025, as Brazil's fiscal plan disappoints

The US currency rises 1.58% in spot trading amid concerns over Brazil's 2027 fiscal measures and US interest rates.

What happened

The US dollar closed Thursday, November 12, 2026, up 1.58% against the Brazilian real at R$5.6507 in spot selling, according to Reuters, its highest closing level since June 2, 2025. Investors reacted negatively to the Brazilian government's proposed fiscal measures for 2027, while the dollar also strengthened internationally. Planning Minister Bruno Moretti said the measures were expected to have a R$50 billion to R$60 billion fiscal impact in 2027. Folha de S.Paulo separately reported the currency's rise and a decline in the Ibovespa stock index. The spot-market closing rate differs from the Central Bank's Ptax reference rate, which is calculated using a separate methodology.

The Crow's analysis

The exchange rate reflects both Brazilian fiscal risk perceptions and global interest-rate expectations. Some investors question whether the proposed measures will stabilize public debt, while stronger US inflation and interest-rate expectations support the dollar internationally. A sustained weakening of the real could increase import costs, though the effect on consumer prices depends on the duration of the move and other factors. The fiscal proposal still requires legislative approval.

Counterpoint

The government announces tens of billions in fiscal measures, but markets still want to know where the structural adjustment is. Exchange rates respond to numbers, risks and expectations, not just speeches. Blaming Brasília alone would also ignore the dollar's broader international strength.
