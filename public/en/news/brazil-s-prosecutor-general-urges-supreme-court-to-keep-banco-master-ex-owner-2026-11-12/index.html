Brazil's prosecutor general urges Supreme Court to keep Banco Master ex-owner detained

Paulo Gonet filed an opinion on November 11 opposing Daniel Vorcaro's release; the court has not yet ruled.

What happened

Brazil's prosecutor general, Paulo Gonet, submitted an opinion to the Supreme Federal Court (STF, Brazil's Supreme Court) on November 11, 2026, supporting continued pretrial detention of Daniel Vorcaro, Banco Master's former controlling shareholder. The filing responds to a defense request for release and to a request from Justice Edson Fachin. Vorcaro has been detained since March 2026 and is held at the Papudinha facility in the Federal District. The Prosecutor General's Office (PGR) argues that risks to public order, evidence gathering and enforcement of criminal law remain. The investigation concerns alleged irregularities in loan portfolios traded between Banco Master and Banco de Brasília (BRB). Federal Police indicted Vorcaro and other suspects in early November, but an indictment is not a conviction. The PGR also addressed requests by other suspects. The opinion is not a judicial ruling and the Supreme Court has not decided whether to release him.

The Crow's analysis

Prosecutors cite the seriousness of the alleged wrongdoing, Vorcaro's purported influence, unrecovered assets and suspicions of interference with investigators. Allegations of intimidation, access to confidential information and influence over officials remain under investigation and must not be presented as established criminal guilt. The defense disputes detention, alleges excessive delay and says no official expert report has established losses to BRB in the loan-portfolio transaction. The Supreme Court must assess the current grounds for pretrial detention, the investigation's complexity and the presumption of innocence. The case raises broader concerns about banking supervision and institutional integrity.

Counterpoint

The Banco Master case cannot become a contest of narratives: serious allegations are not a verdict, but they cannot be swept aside either. Prosecutors must demonstrate any concrete risk to evidence before the Supreme Court, and the defense's objections deserve legal rather than political answers. Investigating possible fraud transparently and respecting the presumption of innocence are equally essential.
