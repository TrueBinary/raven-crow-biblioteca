Saudi Arabia says it intercepted three Houthi missiles targeting Riyadh and two drones

The Saudi-led coalition attributes the launches to Yemen's Houthis; no casualties or damage were reported in the statements.

What happened

On Wednesday, November 11, 2026, the Saudi-led coalition said its air defenses had intercepted and destroyed three cruise missiles launched by Yemen's Houthis toward the Riyadh region. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki subsequently said two drones had also been intercepted while allegedly heading toward Mecca and southern parts of the country. Reuters, AFP and Anadolu reported the announcements. Officials provided no details about casualties or damage from these incidents. The reported trajectories and targets are coalition claims and have not been fully independently verified.

The Crow's analysis

The announcement follows interceptions reported on Tuesday and comes amid renewed fighting between the Houthis and Saudi-backed forces. Threats to cities, airports and oil infrastructure increase risks to civilians and energy supplies. The conflict also involves Saudi airstrikes in Yemen, raising humanitarian concerns. Its proximity to Red Sea shipping routes gives the escalation wider regional consequences. Wednesday's reported launches are separate from those reported on November 10.

Counterpoint

The claims about targets and interceptions come from the Saudi-led coalition, a party to the conflict. The Houthis have previously denied allegations that they targeted Mecca and Medina. Not every detail has been independently verified, and no casualties or damage should be inferred from the statements. Saudi attacks on Houthi positions in Yemen are also part of the broader context.
