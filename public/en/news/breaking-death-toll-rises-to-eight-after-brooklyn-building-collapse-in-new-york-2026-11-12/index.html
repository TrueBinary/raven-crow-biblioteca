BREAKINGDeath toll rises to eight after Brooklyn building collapse in New York

Rescuers continue searching the rubble; at least 17 people were injured, according to Thursday's updated figures.

What happened

Eight people have died after a three-story building collapsed in Flatbush, Brooklyn, New York, according to a November 12, 2026 update attributed to Mayor Zohran Mamdani and reported by the Associated Press and The Washington Post. The building fell on Wednesday afternoon, November 11, beside a site where excavation work was underway for a new development. At least 17 people were injured, including emergency responders. More than 250 firefighters, police officers and rescuers searched the rubble using dogs and hand tools. A woman and a family with two children were rescued alive. The cause remains under investigation; the adjacent excavation has not been established as the cause. Earlier Reuters and AFP reports cited two deaths, before AP and The Washington Post reported the higher toll. The figures may change as searches continue.

The Crow's analysis

The excavation next door and the fact that debris fell into the construction pit are important investigative details, but neither establishes responsibility without engineering findings. Emergency teams warned that unstable rubble created further danger during rescue work. Rapidly changing casualty counts also show why early headlines require careful updating. The disaster raises questions about structural inspections and safeguards around construction sites in densely populated neighborhoods.

Counterpoint

A city that keeps building upward must ensure the ground does not give way beneath its existing residents. Demanding inspections and accountability is reasonable; blaming the neighboring excavation before a technical investigation is not. The immediate priorities are finding survivors and determining what went wrong.
