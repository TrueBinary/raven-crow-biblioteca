COP31 begins in Türkiye amid pressure for concrete climate action

The UN climate conference runs from November 9 to 20 in Antalya, with finance, adaptation and the energy transition high on the agenda.

What happened

The 31st Conference of the Parties to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (COP31) begins on Monday, November 9, 2026, in Antalya, Türkiye, and is scheduled to run until November 20. The United Nations and the convention secretariat confirm the dates and venue. Türkiye holds the conference presidency, led by Environment Minister Murat Kurum, while Australia's Climate Change Minister Chris Bowen chairs negotiations under a bilateral arrangement. Delegates are gathering to discuss emissions reductions, adaptation to global warming, climate finance and implementation of earlier commitments. According to the Turkish presidency's official programme, the first day's theme is food, agriculture and health; the summit of heads of state and government is scheduled for November 11 and 12. The Associated Press reports that the meeting follows a year of extreme weather amid international energy tensions. As of Monday morning, the ceremonial opening was still scheduled for 11 a.m. local time, so this report does not claim that the ceremony has already taken place.

The Crow's analysis

COP31's central test is whether international targets and declarations can become funded, measurable policies. Vulnerable countries are seeking support to adapt cities, infrastructure and agriculture to heatwaves, floods and droughts, while governments disagree over the pace of the shift away from fossil fuels. The Turkish presidency emphasizes implementation, electrification and methane reductions. The shared leadership arrangement with Australia will require coordination between the formal presidency and technical negotiations. Concrete outcomes will depend on verifiable commitments and the willingness to fund them, not just final statements.

Counterpoint

Climate summits have a record of slow implementation, and energy transitions carry different costs and priorities for industrialized, emerging and fossil-fuel-exporting economies. Disagreements over timelines, responsibility and funding are substantial. COP31's convening does not itself mean any new agreement has been reached.
