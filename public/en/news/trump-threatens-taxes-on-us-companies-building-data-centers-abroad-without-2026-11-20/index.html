TRUMP THREATENS TAXES ON US COMPANIES BUILDING DATA CENTERS ABROAD, WITHOUT POLICY DETAILS

The US president threatens penalties for overseas data centers but has yet to present a formal proposal.

What happened

US President Donald Trump said at a White House news conference in Washington on November 20, 2026, that he intends to impose heavy taxes on American companies building data centers overseas, Reuters and the Associated Press reported. Trump specified no tax rate, legal instrument or enforcement mechanism and said he had not yet briefed his own staff, according to the Associated Press. Reuters reported that US rules announced on November 16, 2026, restricted remote access to advanced artificial intelligence chips and gave certain data centers serving China-based firms 90 days to meet licensing requirements. These are separate developments: the chip restrictions have been announced, while the proposed tax remains an unformalized threat.

The Crow's analysis

The statement exposes a tension between keeping computing investment inside the United States and locating services near international users. Data centers underpin AI training and operation but also support latency-sensitive services such as payments. A penalty could affect expansion costs and plans, although its scope remains undefined. The backdrop includes competition with China and US community concerns about electricity and water use. There is no basis yet to conclude that specific Brazilian projects will be affected.

Counterpoint

A presidential statement is not an enacted tax. Rates, legal authority, implementation and exemptions remain unspecified. The November 16 chip-access restrictions are distinct from the threatened November 20 tax; precise financial impacts cannot yet be established.
