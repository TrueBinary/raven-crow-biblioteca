Russia says it shot down 92 drones over Leningrad region near strategic ports

Regional governor reports overnight interceptions; Reuters says it could not independently verify the figure.

What happened

Early on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, Leningrad region Governor Aleksandr Drozdenko said Russian air defenses had shot down 92 drones in the northwestern region. The number is a Russian official claim, not an independently verified count: Reuters explicitly said it could not confirm the report. The region includes the Baltic ports of Ust-Luga, Primorsk and Vysotsk, important to Russian oil and grain exports, and the Kirishi oil refinery. The announcement follows a November 9 drone strike that set part of the Kirishi refinery on fire; authorities also reported that a woman was killed in the region. This reporting does not independently establish that the 92 drones hit the ports or caused new damage to facilities.

The Crow's analysis

The alert underscores the exposure of Russian logistics corridors along the Baltic, which matter to export revenue and energy supply. An official interception tally alone cannot establish the true scale of an attack or the intended target of each aircraft. The episode comes as Russia, Ukraine and the United States resume negotiations in Abu Dhabi after months of deadlock. Continued military activity alongside diplomacy highlights the gap between talks and a reduction in hostilities. The reported events on November 11 should not be confused with the separately documented Kirishi refinery fire on November 9.

Counterpoint

Russian regional authorities say air defenses intercepted the drones, but the figure of 92 has not been independently verified. It should not be presented as a confirmed number of Ukrainian aircraft lost, nor as evidence that ports or oil installations were destroyed. The origin of each drone has not been sufficiently established in this reporting. Later official and independent accounts may revise the tally.
