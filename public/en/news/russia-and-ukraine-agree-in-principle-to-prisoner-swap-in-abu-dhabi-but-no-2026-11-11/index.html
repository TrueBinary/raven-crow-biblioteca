Russia and Ukraine agree in principle to prisoner swap in Abu Dhabi, but no ceasefire emerges

After two days of US-mediated talks, delegations announce a planned humanitarian exchange; the number of prisoners and timetable remain unsettled.

What happened

Russian and Ukrainian representatives concluded two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates, on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. The delegations announced an agreement in principle on a large-scale prisoner-of-war exchange, but no breakthrough toward ending the war and no ceasefire. US envoy Jared Kushner said the final number of prisoners would be determined later. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had said the parties discussed swapping 1,000 prisoners from each side, potentially 1,500 each, but those figures are not finalized. Reuters and the Associated Press independently reported the outcome.

The Crow's analysis

The humanitarian understanding is a tangible result amid continuing disagreements over occupied territory, Ukraine's security guarantees and Europe's participation in future negotiations. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner plan to travel to Moscow to meet Vladimir Putin and later see Zelenskyy. A prisoner swap agreement is not a peace treaty: fighting continues and the sides remain far apart on Donbas and Europe's security architecture.

Counterpoint

Russian and Ukrainian officials described some progress, but the US mediator acknowledged deep mistrust. Moscow continues to demand territory that Kyiv refuses to surrender and questions European involvement in the trilateral format. Ukraine and European partners seek credible security guarantees and a say in decisions affecting Europe. Until the numbers, schedule and actual releases are confirmed, this remains an agreement in principle, not a completed exchange.
