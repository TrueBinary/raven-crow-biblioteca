Putin threatens foreign troops in Ukraine during new US-led peace talks

Russia's president rejects European forces under a potential settlement; US envoys suggest Europe could join future talks, but no agreement has been confirmed.

What happened

Russian President Vladimir Putin said on November 12, 2026, that foreign troops deployed in Ukraine after a possible peace settlement could trigger renewed war and would be treated as targets, according to Reuters, the Associated Press and The Guardian. Putin met US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in Moscow. Witkoff said European representatives could join the next round of talks, but Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov did not confirm the arrangement, while the Associated Press reported Russian opposition to European participation. The envoys are expected to travel to Dublin for talks with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Major disagreements remain over occupied territory, security guarantees and European troops. No ceasefire or final settlement has been announced. Zelenskyy also reported new restrictions on the Starshield military communications service affecting Ukrainian operations; the cause of the disruptions has not been independently established.

The Crow's analysis

The talks show the gap between reopening dialogue and reaching a workable settlement. Russia continues to demand territory and rejects European troops as a security guarantee, while Ukraine opposes surrendering land it still controls. European participation remains disputed: Witkoff's remarks are not a formally confirmed commitment from Moscow. Without agreement on borders and security, further meetings may sustain diplomacy without delivering immediate peace.

Counterpoint

Threatening to attack foreign forces while discussing peace is an unusual form of diplomacy: the negotiating table comes with an artillery warning. Still, testing verifiable proposals is better than selling empty optimism. Any settlement must address Ukrainians' security and cannot mistake military occupation for consent.
