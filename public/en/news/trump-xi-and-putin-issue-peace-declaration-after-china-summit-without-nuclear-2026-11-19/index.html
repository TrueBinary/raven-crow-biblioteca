TRUMP, XI AND PUTIN ISSUE PEACE DECLARATION AFTER CHINA SUMMIT, WITHOUT NUCLEAR DEAL

Joint text calls for dialogue on nuclear risks, UN reform and energy but sets no disarmament targets.

What happened

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin met on Thursday, November 19, 2026, in Shenzhen, China, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit. Their governments subsequently issued a joint declaration reaffirming that nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought. The text calls for dialogue on nuclear risk reduction, reform of the United Nations (UN), increased supplies of refined petroleum products and respect for the immunity of state officials. No nuclear treaty, verifiable arsenal-reduction targets or agreement to end the war in Ukraine was announced.

The Crow's analysis

The new development, beyond the previously announced summit, is the release of a common statement. A promise of dialogue is not disarmament: the document provides no timetable, inspection mechanism or quantified commitments. The meeting followed the expiration of the US-Russia New START treaty, as Washington seeks to bring China into future negotiations. It also groups very different subjects — nuclear security, energy and international courts — without detailing implementation. Tangible results will depend on subsequent, verifiable commitments.

Counterpoint

The Associated Press described the meeting as largely symbolic, without major breakthroughs. Ukraine and European allies were absent despite discussions of the war, prompting European criticism of decisions about Kyiv without its participation. Disagreements over Ukraine, Taiwan, trade and arms control persist. The declaration records intentions, not guaranteed results.
