IRAN LAUNCHES DRONES AT US MILITARY RADAR IN BAHRAIN; AUTHORITIES SAY ATTACK WAS INTERCEPTED

Iran's Revolutionary Guards claim an operation against Sheikh Isa Air Base, while Bahrain and US officials say air defenses stopped it.

What happened

On November 17, 2026, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it launched drones toward a US military radar system at Sheikh Isa Air Base in Bahrain. Bahrain and US officials said the attack was intercepted. The Guards described it as retaliation for alleged US military drone incursions into Iranian airspace. Independent confirmation of damage to the radar was not available; the Associated Press reported no known injuries or damage.

The Crow's analysis

The declared target matters because Bahrain hosts US military facilities near Gulf shipping routes vital to global oil and gas trade. A drone launch, a successful penetration of air defenses and confirmed damage to radar are three distinct outcomes. The Associated Press described this as part of a recent series of attacks, following reported incidents on the preceding Friday and Sunday. Repeated strikes increase the danger of miscalculation and regional shipping disruption.

Counterpoint

Iran's claim establishes what its forces say they targeted, not whether the equipment was hit. Bahrain and the US reported interception, but independent evidence is insufficient to assess every defensive engagement. Separate maritime incidents should not automatically be attributed to Iran without investigation.
