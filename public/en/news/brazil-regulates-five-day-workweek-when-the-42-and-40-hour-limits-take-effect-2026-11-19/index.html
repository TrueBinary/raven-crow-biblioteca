BRAZIL REGULATES FIVE-DAY WORKWEEKWHEN THE 42- AND 40-HOUR LIMITS TAKE EFFECT

A November 19 decree sets out paid rest, hourly pay calculations and special schedules; the transition starts December 16.

What happened

Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva signed Decree No. 13,173 in Brasília on November 19, 2026. Published in a special edition of the federal gazette, it implements Constitutional Amendment No. 141 of October 16, 2026. The amendment provides for two paid weekly rest days and a gradual reduction in the maximum workweek from 44 to 42 hours on December 16, 2026, and to 40 hours on December 16, 2027, without cutting monthly salaries. The Ministry of Labor and Employment (MTE) is to issue supplementary rules.

The Crow's analysis

For employees on fixed monthly pay, the divisor used to calculate hourly wages changes. The usual divisor for 44 hours is 220; the decree specifies 210 for 42 hours and 200 for 40 hours. As a purely arithmetic example, a R$2,200 monthly salary means R$10 per hour at 220, approximately R$10.48 at 210, and R$11 at 200. This may raise the base for overtime pay but does not automatically increase monthly salary. The MTE estimates that 14.8 million workers on six-day schedules and 21.7 million already on five-day schedules but working 44 hours will be affected. The change stems from the constitutional amendment passed in October, not from the decree alone.

Counterpoint

Individual implementation will depend on employment contracts, collective agreements, special work arrangements and further MTE guidance. The decree also addresses rotating shifts and 12-hour-on/36-hour-off schedules, which should not be treated as an automatic switch to a standard five-day week. The worker figures are government estimates, not a count of contracts already changed.
