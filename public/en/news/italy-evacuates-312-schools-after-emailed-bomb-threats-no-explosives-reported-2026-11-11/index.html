Italy evacuates 312 schools after emailed bomb threats; no explosives reported

Anonymous messages also reach schools in the UK and Ireland; Italy's government says there is no evidence of a concrete threat.

What happened

Italian authorities evacuated 312 schools on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, following anonymous emailed bomb threats. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said there was no indication of an actual danger and that students were safe as police checked the premises. At the time of reporting, no explosives had been found and no injuries linked to the threats were reported. Similar messages reached schools in Scotland, Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland. Some local authorities treated the alerts as hoaxes without ordering evacuations. Reuters and the Associated Press independently reported the incidents.

The Crow's analysis

The alerts disrupted education and required a substantial police response. Similar threats across borders call for coordination, but their resemblance does not establish a common perpetrator, foreign involvement or the presence of explosive devices. Authorities must balance precautionary safety measures, continuity of classes and clear public communication.

Counterpoint

Italy's government said it had no evidence of a concrete danger while inspections continued as a precaution. Investigators have not established who sent the messages. Claims of responsibility by particular organizations or foreign states remain unproven.
