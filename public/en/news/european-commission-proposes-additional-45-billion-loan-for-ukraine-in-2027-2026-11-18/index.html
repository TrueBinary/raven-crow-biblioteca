European Commission proposes additional €45 billion loan for Ukraine in 2027

Plan earmarks €25 billion for defence and €20 billion for budget support, pending EU approvals.

What happened

On November 18, 2026, the European Commission unveiled legislative proposals for up to €45 billion in additional loans to Ukraine in 2027. The amount would cover roughly two-thirds of an estimated €70 billion additional financing gap. Up to €25 billion would fund military assistance and €20 billion general budget support. The EU would borrow on capital markets backed by its budget, with principal repayable by Ukraine only after Russia pays reparations. The plan has not yet been approved.

The Crow's analysis

This is a proposed financing mechanism, not an immediate disbursement. It is additional to the previously agreed €90 billion EU support loan for 2026–27. The €45 billion would cover about 64% of the estimated additional €70 billion need, leaving approximately €25 billion to other partners. Budget assistance would be tied to rule-of-law and anti-corruption reforms. The EU budget guarantee, rather than the repayment promise alone, is central to understanding the financial exposure.

Counterpoint

EU governments and the European Parliament must still approve the measures, and the budget-guarantee amendment requires unanimity. Russian reparations have no assured payment date. The funding gap may also change as the war evolves.
