Guterres warns at COP31 that climate crisis is advancing faster than governments' response

UN chief calls for faster emissions cuts and a shift away from fossil fuels; countries discuss electrification, but the proposal does not mandate an end to oil, gas and coal.

What happened

On November 11, 2026, at the COP31 leaders' summit in Antalya, Türkiye, United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres warned that the climate crisis was advancing faster than the international response and called for faster emissions reductions and a transition away from fossil fuels. Reuters reported his remarks as negotiators from nearly 200 countries gathered. The conference runs from November 9 to 20, according to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). Türkiye is promoting the '35 by 35' initiative, which aims to increase electricity's share of global energy use to 35% by 2035. However, the electrification proposal does not require all electricity to come from clean sources and does not establish a binding deadline for ending oil, gas and coal use. On November 10, the UN also reported discussions of Brazil's voluntary energy-transition roadmap, which does not automatically impose legal obligations on other countries.

The Crow's analysis

The speech shifts attention from broad targets to implementation, finance and infrastructure. Greater electrification of transport, heating and industry can lower emissions when accompanied by cleaner power generation; electrification alone does not guarantee decarbonization. Brazil's roadmap presented at COP31 seeks to guide national plans for reducing fossil-fuel dependence, but implementation remains with governments. The UN itself identifies inadequate power grids and high financing costs as major obstacles. Guterres' call for urgency contrasts with the difficulty of turning diplomatic declarations into verifiable measures and legally enforceable commitments.

Counterpoint

Oil- and gas-producing countries and economies reliant on fossil fuels argue that a rapid transition must account for employment, energy security, prices and investment capacity. Türkiye's electrification initiative aims to expand energy alternatives, but critics note that it neither requires clean power nor mandates a fossil-fuel phaseout. At the time of reporting, COP31 had not adopted a new binding agreement on these points. Guterres' warning is a UN assessment and political appeal, not a decision already adopted by participating countries.
