Russian strike hits school food warehouse in Odesa, killing three, Ukrainian officials say

Two women and a man were killed and another woman was seriously wounded, Ukrainian authorities said, ahead of renewed peace talks.

What happened

A strike attributed to Russian forces hit a warehouse storing food for nurseries and schools in Odesa, Ukraine, on Monday, November 9, 2026. Regional military administration head Oleh Kiper reported that three people — two women and one man — were killed and another woman was seriously wounded. City military administration chief Serhii Lysak also confirmed three deaths and said emergency crews were fighting a fire at the warehouse. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 317 drones overnight, of which 271 were shot down or suppressed, with impacts recorded at 31 locations. The attack came ahead of US-mediated talks between Ukrainian and Russian representatives scheduled for November 10–11 in Abu Dhabi. The warehouse details were reported by Interfax-Ukraine and Euromaidan Press, citing Ukrainian officials.

The Crow's analysis

A strike on a facility supplying nurseries and schools, if its civilian function is confirmed, could disrupt essential services beyond the immediate casualties. Continued attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure also raise the risk of disruptions to electricity and food supplies as winter approaches. The timing ahead of diplomatic talks does not, on its own, establish that the strike was intended to influence negotiations. Rescue operations and investigations will be needed to establish the full impact and the nature of the target.

Counterpoint

The death toll, injuries and warehouse description are attributed to Ukrainian authorities, as reported by two outlets; not all operational details have been independently verified on the ground. No specific military rationale from Moscow for this particular strike has been established publicly. The accompanying 2011 archive photograph shows central Odesa, not the warehouse or the attack.
