France summons Iranian ambassador over cyberattack on 700 supermarkets

Paris blames Iran-linked Revolutionary Guard operatives for refrigeration sabotage and seeks European sanctions; Tehran rejects the allegation.

What happened

France's Foreign Ministry confirmed on November 10, 2026 that it summoned Iran's ambassador in Paris after attributing cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration controls to operatives linked to Iran's Revolutionary Guard. According to France's official November 9 statement, the attacks took place overnight on October 11–12 and targeted controllers at more than 700 retail sites, causing food losses, property damage and some fires. Paris prosecutors opened an investigation. Reuters confirmed the ambassador's summons on Tuesday, while the Associated Press separately reported the accusations and damage. Iranian responsibility remains an attribution by French authorities, not a final judicial finding.

The Crow's analysis

The episode illustrates how connected refrigeration systems can be targeted for sabotage, with economic and public-health consequences. France plans to seek European sanctions and has warned it may take further proportionate steps to deter attacks. Industrial cybersecurity is increasingly a foreign-policy and supply-chain issue. Investigators still need to establish the command structure and full scale of the damage, and official attribution must be distinguished from a final court ruling.

Counterpoint

Iran rejected France's accusation, according to Reuters. No final judicial ruling has established the suspects' responsibility. Any sanctions and investigations should be assessed against disclosed evidence and due process.
