RUSSIAN STRIKES HIT THREE KYIV BRIDGES; AUTHORITIES REPORT THREE DEAD

Overnight missile attacks disrupt crossings over the Dnipro River; casualty figures and the extent of damage remain preliminary.

What happened

On Friday, November 20, 2026, Russian missile strikes hit three bridges across the Dnipro River in Kyiv, Ukraine's capital. Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported three deaths and about 15 injuries. Traffic was halted or restricted on the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi, Darnytskyi and Pivdennyi bridges as crews assessed the damage. Fires and damage to other civilian buildings and infrastructure were also reported. Reuters and the Financial Times covered the attacks; initial casualty figures and structural assessments remain subject to updates.

The Crow's analysis

The Dnipro crossings connect Kyiv's eastern and western banks and are essential to commuting, emergency response and freight. Their disruption can extend beyond physical damage: a bridge that remains standing may still be closed for safety inspections, forcing traffic onto alternative routes. The latest strikes follow attacks on Kyiv bridges earlier in the week and Ukrainian strikes on the bridge connecting Russia to annexed Crimea. The sequence puts transport infrastructure under further pressure but does not, on its own, establish the intent behind each strike.

Counterpoint

Early casualty figures and bridge damage reports came from Ukrainian authorities. This report does not have a final engineering assessment or independent verification of every reported injury and damaged structure. Moscow says it targets military and logistical infrastructure, which does not establish the military status of every site struck. International humanitarian law requires distinction between military objectives and civilian objects, proportionality and precautions in attack.
