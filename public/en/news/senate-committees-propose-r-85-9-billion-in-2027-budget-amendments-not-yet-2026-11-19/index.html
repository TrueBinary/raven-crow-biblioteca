SENATE COMMITTEES PROPOSE R$85.9 BILLION IN 2027 BUDGET AMENDMENTS, NOT YET APPROVED

Sixteen committees backed 96 proposed appropriations; requests for healthcare, education and sports still face budget scrutiny.

What happened

According to a report published by Brazil's Senate news agency, Agência Senado, on November 19, 2026, 16 Senate and congressional committees in Brasília approved 96 proposed appropriation amendments on November 17 and 18, totaling R$85.9 billion for the 2027 budget. Agência Senado also records seven reallocation amendments worth R$696 million and requests including R$14.5 billion from the Social Affairs Committee, R$12.75 billion from the Sports Committee and R$6.9 billion from the Education Committee. The Joint Budget Committee (CMO) must still review the proposals; they are not authorized or disbursed spending.

The Crow's analysis

The R$85.9 billion figure measures requests, not an automatic increase in public spending. Appropriation amendments identify spending and an offset in reserves or other budget lines; reallocation amendments transfer funding from specific appropriations. For readers, the distinction is between proposing, authorizing and actually spending money. The 2027 annual budget bill, PLN 24/2026, is overseen by rapporteur Eduardo Braga, while the budget guidelines bill, PLN 2/2026, sets fiscal rules and priorities. For example, the Sports Committee's proposed R$50 million for a Federal University of Sport is a request, not a guaranteed transfer or construction project.

Counterpoint

Committee endorsement does not mean Congress has passed the budget. Senator Eduardo Gomes said there is no dedicated reserve for committee amendments; the preliminary budget report will determine how they can be accommodated. The announced timetable calls for the Joint Budget Committee's final report on December 16 and a congressional vote on December 17, but dates and amounts may change. A separate R$44.8 billion provision for mandatory individual and state-delegation amendments should not be confused with the R$85.9 billion in committee proposals.
