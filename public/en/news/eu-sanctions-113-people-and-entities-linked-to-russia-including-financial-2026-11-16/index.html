EU SANCTIONS 113 PEOPLE AND ENTITIES LINKED TO RUSSIA, INCLUDING FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS

The EU Council targets nearly 50 Russian banks and financial institutions alongside oil, e-commerce and shadow-fleet networks.

What happened

On Monday, November 16, 2026, the Council of the European Union announced sanctions against 12 individuals and 101 entities connected to sectors it says help finance Russia's war against Ukraine. The new designations include nearly 50 Russian banks and financial institutions, as well as energy businesses, shadow-fleet-linked operators and people connected with the Wildberries and Ozon online retail businesses. The figure of 113 refers to all individuals and entities newly listed, not to the number of banks. The measures were announced in Brussels and enacted through legal instruments published in the Official Journal of the European Union.

The Crow's analysis

Individual designations are not a blanket trade embargo. Listed parties face asset freezes within EU jurisdiction and restrictions on receiving funds or economic resources from EU persons; listed individuals also face EU travel bans. Targeting banks seeks to disrupt financial channels, while targeting oil buyers and shipping intermediaries addresses networks that may help Russian exports continue despite earlier restrictions. The real-world impact depends on each party's exposure to the European financial system and enforcement. Businesses should check the actual sanctions list before assuming that every Russia-related transaction is forbidden.

Counterpoint

The Council says the measures respond to Russian attacks on Ukrainian civilian infrastructure and seek to reduce war funding. The immediate economic impact has not been established by the announcement. An EU asset freeze does not automatically seize property outside EU jurisdiction or halt all activity by those listed. Reuters reported that Wildberries said it had no EU assets or operations and expected no operational impact; this is the company's claim, not independent evidence that the measures will be ineffective.
