US PROPOSES 10% TARIFF ON BRAZILIAN AIRCRAFT; BRAZIL OBJECTS

Washington also proposes higher out-of-quota sugar duties; Brasília disputes the measures and remains open to negotiations.

What happened

On November 25, 2026, in Washington, the Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) announced a consultation on new barriers to imports of Brazilian aircraft and sugar. That same day, in Brasília, Brazil's government objected to the proposal and said it would continue seeking a negotiated solution. According to USTR, the proposed additional duty on finished Brazilian aircraft is 10%, ending the existing exemption under the Section 301 trade action. The office also said written comments are due December 30, 2026, with a hearing scheduled for January 14, 2027. Out-of-quota sugar imports are also targeted by the proposal, which remains under consultation.

The Crow's analysis

Aircraft manufacturers face the prospect of higher costs to access the US market. How the burden is shared among producers, buyers and other participants will depend on contracts and pricing negotiations. Changes to out-of-quota sugar duties could alter competition among supplier countries. The US trade process began in 2025 and also concerns Brazilian regulatory issues, including digital payments. Brasília's response shows that the dispute extends beyond the two products and remains part of broader trade negotiations.

Counterpoint

Washington argues that the measures would strengthen its leverage over Brazilian practices it considers harmful to US commerce. Brasília disagrees and says it has negotiated in good faith, without ruling out World Trade Organization (WTO) proceedings or reciprocal measures. The proposed duties are not confirmed as final; the consultation precedes a decision.
