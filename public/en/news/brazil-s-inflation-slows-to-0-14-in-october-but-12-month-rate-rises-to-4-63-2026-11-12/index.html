Brazil's inflation slows to 0.14% in October, but 12-month rate rises to 4.63%

The IPCA comes in below forecasts; food and beverages rise 1.04% while annual inflation remains above the target ceiling.

What happened

Brazil's national statistics agency, IBGE, reported on November 12, 2026 that the Broad National Consumer Price Index (IPCA) rose 0.14% in October, down from 0.82% in September. The figure was below the 0.25% forecast in a Reuters survey. However, inflation over 12 months increased from 4.58% to 4.63%, exceeding the Central Bank's 4.5% target ceiling. Food and beverages rose 1.04%, while housing fell 0.51% and transport declined 0.59%, according to data reported by Reuters. The headline index is an average and does not imply that prices fell across the board.

The Crow's analysis

The softer monthly reading offers some relief but does not erase pressure on household budgets. Rising food prices contrast with declines in housing and transport, helping explain why consumers may experience inflation differently from the national average. With the annual rate still above the tolerance band, monetary policy remains under scrutiny. One monthly reading alone does not establish that an immediate cut in the Selic benchmark rate is warranted.

Counterpoint

The headline rate slowed, but grocery bills did not get the memo: food and beverages rose 1.04%. Acknowledging the improvement does not mean claiming prices are falling across the board. The real test is purchasing power, borrowing costs and the fiscal outlook, not a convenient headline.
