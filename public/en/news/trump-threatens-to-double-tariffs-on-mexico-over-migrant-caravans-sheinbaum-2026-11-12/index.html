Trump threatens to double tariffs on Mexico over migrant caravans; Sheinbaum rules out repression

US president threatens a tariff increase unless Mexico stops groups traveling north; Mexico says no caravan has reached the US border since January 2025.

What happened

On Thursday, November 12, 2026, US President Donald Trump threatened to immediately double tariffs on Mexican goods unless Mexico stops migrant caravans heading toward the US border. He made the threat on Truth Social, but no tariff increase had been confirmed as implemented. Reuters reported that roughly 1,500 people, mostly Central Americans, departed Tapachula in southern Mexico on Tuesday. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum responded at her morning news conference that no caravan had reached Mexico's northern border since Trump took office in January 2025, and ruled out using repression against migrants. She said officials explain US entry restrictions to the groups and offer options for returning home. Estimates of caravan size vary by source, location and time.

The Crow's analysis

The warning raises US pressure on Mexico amid tariff negotiations and talks about the North American trade framework. As of the reports, the threatened increase had not taken effect, and the legal mechanism for doubling duties was uncertain. Sheinbaum's response seeks to demonstrate migration management without repression. The differing estimates require caution: the number leaving Tapachula is not necessarily the number still traveling. For companies and consumers, a possible tariff escalation adds uncertainty to closely integrated supply chains.

Counterpoint

Threatening to double tariffs over a caravan turns a migration dispute into a trade bill. Mexico, in turn, needs verifiable data showing whether its information and return policy works. Between ultimatums and speeches, consumers may end up paying for a tariff escalation.
