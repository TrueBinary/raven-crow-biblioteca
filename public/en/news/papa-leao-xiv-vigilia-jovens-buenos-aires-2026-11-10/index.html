Pope Leo XIV draws estimated million to Argentina youth vigil, warns against worship of fame

At a Buenos Aires prayer vigil, the pontiff urges young people not to make money, looks or social-media popularity their idols.

What happened

On the evening of November 10, 2026, Pope Leo XIV attended a prayer vigil with young people at the Monumento de los Españoles in Buenos Aires during his Argentina visit. Reuters and Associated Press reported an estimated attendance of around 1 million. He urged young people not to idolize money, success, appearance or social-media popularity, and to follow examples of holiness. Earlier, he celebrated Mass in Córdoba, where attendance was estimated at 650,000. The Vatican scheduled the vigil for 8:15 p.m. local time. The crowd estimates were not independently audited counts.

The Crow's analysis

The pope's message brings digital culture and the pressure for public recognition into a broader moral and spiritual discussion about personal priorities. The large turnout highlights the Catholic Church's ability to mobilize people in Argentina, but it does not by itself establish a lasting change in religious practice. His warning about the worship of metrics and prestige also resonates with content creators and social-media audiences.

Counterpoint

The pope's criticism of obsessive fame-seeking is not a blanket condemnation of technology, work or social media. Reported crowd sizes are estimates rather than audited totals. The visit also takes place amid Argentine social and economic debates; pastoral statements should not automatically be read as endorsements of a party or candidate.
