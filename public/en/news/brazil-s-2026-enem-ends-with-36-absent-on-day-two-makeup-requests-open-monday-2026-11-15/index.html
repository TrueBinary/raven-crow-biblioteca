BRAZIL'S 2026 ENEM ENDS WITH 36% ABSENT ON DAY TWO; MAKEUP REQUESTS OPEN MONDAY

Preliminary figures show higher absenteeism, while eligible candidates have until November 20 to request a makeup exam.

What happened

On November 15, 2026, the second day of Brazil's National High School Exam (Enem) recorded a preliminary absentee rate of 36%, according to the Ministry of Education (MEC) and the National Institute for Educational Studies and Research (Inep). The rate was 32% on the first day, November 8. Power outages affected 11 testing locations: four in Minas Gerais, three in Bahia, two in Paraná, one in Maranhão and one in Piauí. Candidates affected by eligible logistical problems, natural disasters or infectious diseases may request a makeup exam through the Participant Page from November 16 to 20, with supporting documentation. Makeup tests are scheduled for December 15 and 16. Official answer keys are due November 18, and individual results on January 18, 2027.

The Crow's analysis

Absenteeism rose four percentage points between the two Sundays. Compared with the 30% reported for day two in 2025, the increase was six percentage points, not a 6% relative rise. MEC partly attributes the change to automatic registration of public-school seniors, which expanded the pool of registered candidates; absentee rates alone therefore cannot measure actual access. According to the ministry, participation among public-school graduates rose from 53.8% in 2025 to 65% in 2026. For candidates whose exams were disrupted, the short application window and evidence requirements are more immediately consequential. Approved candidates retake only the affected day's test.

Counterpoint

These figures are preliminary and do not establish why each absent candidate missed the test. Automatic registration is the ministry's explanation, not a definitive statistical breakdown. Makeup tests are limited to circumstances covered by the rules and require Inep approval.
