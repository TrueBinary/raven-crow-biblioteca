China deployed more than 100 vessels in unannounced drills near Taiwan, officials say

A November 13 report describes Western Pacific maneuvers; Taiwan's defense ministry separately recorded 30 Chinese military aircraft sorties in 24 hours.

What happened

On November 13, 2026, Taiwanese security officials told Reuters that China had deployed more than 100 navy and coast guard vessels during unannounced exercises in the Western Pacific, including areas near Taiwan, earlier in November. The officials described simulated attacks and area-denial operations. Taiwan's Defense Minister Wellington Koo had already confirmed on November 10 that the Liaoning aircraft carrier group and other naval formations were conducting area-denial drills, according to Taiwan's Central News Agency and Deutsche Welle. In its November 13 official bulletin, Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense reported 30 Chinese military aircraft sorties, eight naval ships and three official vessels in the 24 hours ending at 6 a.m. local time. Twenty-three sorties crossed the Taiwan Strait median line or entered areas of Taiwan's air defense identification zone. The figures cover different periods and geographic areas and must not be added together. Beijing has described Liaoning activity as routine training and has not formally confirmed the wider exercise.

The Crow's analysis

Anti-access and area-denial exercises seek to complicate potential intervention by outside forces and can test coordination between naval, air and coast guard units. The reported concentration of more than 100 vessels is attributed to Taiwanese officials and does not independently verify every movement or simulated strike. Taiwan's separate official record of 30 aircraft sorties and 11 vessels in a 24-hour period provides a more narrowly defined account of recent activity. Beijing claims sovereignty over Taiwan; Taiwan's government rejects that claim and says its people must decide their future. Miscalculation and escalation remain key risks.

Counterpoint

A fleet of more than a hundred vessels is hardly a sightseeing cruise. Yet military drills and officials' accounts are not proof that an invasion is imminent. Transparency, independent monitoring and diplomatic caution matter before a show of force turns into a real crisis.
