BRAZIL'S MID-MONTH INFLATION SLOWS TO 0.22% IN NOVEMBER, BUT FOOD PRICES RISE 1.51%

IPCA-15 reaches 4.64% over 12 months; food at home becomes more expensive while transport prices fall.

What happened

Brazil's Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) reported on November 26, 2026, that the country's Broad National Consumer Price Index 15 (IPCA-15) rose 0.22%, compared with 0.32% in October. According to the institute, food and beverage prices increased 1.51%, while transport prices fell 0.71%. The 12-month rate reached 4.64%, up from 4.62% in the previous reading, and tomato prices increased 38.92%, according to IBGE data.

The Crow's analysis

A slowdown in the aggregate index does not mean the cost of living has fallen. Households spending a larger share of income on food may experience different pressures from those reflected in the national average. For monetary policy, the monthly reading offers some relief, but the rise in annual inflation calls for caution.

Counterpoint

The IPCA-15 uses a different collection period from the official IPCA and does not determine interest rates on its own. An increase in one food price does not establish that all prices will move in the same direction.
