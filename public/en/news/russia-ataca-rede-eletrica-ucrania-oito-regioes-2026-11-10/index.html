Russia strikes energy facilities in eight Ukrainian regions, grid operator says

Ukraine's Ukrenergo reports attacks on the power grid and outages in several areas as diplomatic efforts continue.

What happened

On November 10, 2026, Ukraine's state electricity transmission operator Ukrenergo said Russian strikes hit energy facilities in eight Ukrainian regions overnight and into the morning. Reuters identified the affected infrastructure regions as Kyiv, Kharkiv, Sumy, Poltava, Dnipropetrovsk, Vinnytsia, Cherkasy and Kirovohrad. Ukrenergo also reported power outages in parts of Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Kyiv, Sumy, Kharkiv and Chernihiv regions. The lists of regions where facilities were hit and where outages occurred differ because they describe different effects. The Guardian's live coverage also reported attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure and power disruptions on the same day, ahead of diplomatic talks in Abu Dhabi. The figures may change as authorities assess the damage.

The Crow's analysis

Strikes on electricity generation and transmission facilities increase risks to essential services and Ukrainian households as cold weather approaches. Damage in one location can disrupt power in areas that were not directly hit, making the distinction between the two regional lists important. The attacks came as Ukraine, Russia and the United States pursued talks in Abu Dhabi. Continued strikes complicate confidence-building and increase pressure on repair crews and air defences.

Counterpoint

The locations and outages were attributed to Ukrenergo and reported by Reuters; they do not amount to independent confirmation of damage at every facility. Moscow generally describes its military targets as strategic, while Kyiv highlights the impact on civilians. The sources reviewed for this report do not provide a consolidated casualty count specifically for these power-grid strikes. The photograph is an archive image from 2007, not a record of the November 2026 attacks.
