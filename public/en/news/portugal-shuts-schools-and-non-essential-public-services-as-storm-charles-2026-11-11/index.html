Portugal shuts schools and non-essential public services as Storm Charles prompts nationwide alert

Precautionary measures take effect on Wednesday, November 11, across mainland Portugal amid forecasts of heavy rain, dangerous winds and flooding.

What happened

Portugal entered a state of alert across its mainland territory on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, as Storm Charles approached. The government ordered public, private and social-sector educational establishments, including nurseries and preschools, to close, along with non-essential public services. Order No. 13773-A/2026 establishes exceptional measures from midnight to 11:59 p.m. Wednesday, including the suspension of outdoor events and restrictions on access to wooded areas, coastlines, waterways and flood-prone zones. Authorities urged people to avoid non-essential travel. Portugal's meteorological institute, IPMA (Portuguese Institute for Sea and Atmosphere), forecast gusts of up to 140 km/h in exposed areas, according to Agência Brasil, while Reuters cited forecasts reaching 150 km/h. The Associated Press reported largely empty streets in Lisbon and the cancellation of a major technology event for the day. These figures are forecasts from different reports, not confirmed nationwide wind measurements.

The Crow's analysis

The precautionary shutdown aims to limit exposure to strong winds, flooding, falling trees and transport disruption, while keeping emergency resources available. It affects families, businesses, travel and public services, but does not mean essential operations are closed. Firefighters, security forces, healthcare providers and energy and communications teams were placed on heightened readiness. The decision also reflects damage caused by previous storms across the Iberian Peninsula. Subsequent weather and civil protection bulletins will be needed to assess actual impacts rather than anticipated risks.

Counterpoint

The broad closure of schools and public services can create economic costs and logistical difficulties, especially for families with young children and workers unable to work remotely. Portuguese authorities say limiting movement is proportionate to the risk and have encouraged employers to facilitate remote work. At the time of reporting, no definitive death toll, injury count or damage estimate could be attributed to Storm Charles. The alert was announced for November 11 and may be reassessed by authorities.
