Xi Jinping says Taiwan 'reunification' is inevitable; island rejects China's claim

China's president again calls for Taiwan to come under Beijing's rule at a Sun Yat-sen anniversary event amid heightened regional tensions.

What happened

Chinese President Xi Jinping said on Thursday, November 12, 2026, that Taiwan's 'complete reunification' with China must happen and that the tide of history cannot be stopped. He spoke at Beijing's Great Hall of the People during a ceremony marking the 160th anniversary of Sun Yat-sen's birth, commemorating a leading figure in the 1911 revolution. Xi described bringing Taiwan under Beijing's control as a historic mission of the Chinese Communist Party and said he preferred a peaceful process. Democratically governed Taiwan rejects Beijing's sovereignty claim and its proposed 'one country, two systems' framework. The remarks come as G7 foreign ministers warn about Chinese maritime activity east of Taiwan. Xi did not announce a military operation or a timetable for changing the island's status.

The Crow's analysis

Xi's remarks reiterate Beijing's longstanding position, but their timing matters: G7 ministers have warned that Chinese patrols around Taiwan threaten regional stability and freedom of navigation. China regards Taiwan as part of its territory and says it prefers a peaceful outcome, while refusing to rule out force. Taiwan's government under Lai Ching-te says the island's future must be decided by its people. Strong rhetoric should not be confused with an actual invasion announcement: this speech included neither a public attack order nor an annexation schedule.

Counterpoint

When a leader says the destiny of an entire population is inevitable, the question is: inevitable for whom? Beijing calls it reunification; many in Taiwan see the potential loss of their autonomy. Stability in the strait requires negotiations without coercion, not political prophecies. At the same time, a forceful statement is not evidence of an imminent invasion.
