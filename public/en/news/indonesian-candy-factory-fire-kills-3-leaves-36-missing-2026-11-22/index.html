INDONESIAN CANDY FACTORY FIRE KILLS 3, LEAVES 36 MISSING

Rescuers search for 36 missing workers after a candy factory fire in Banten province; three deaths have been confirmed.

What happened

A fire broke out on November 21, 2026, at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia candy factory in Cikande, Serang regency, Banten province, Indonesia, and rescue operations continued on November 22. According to the Associated Press (AP), at least 3 workers died and 36 were still missing in Sunday's reported toll. Reuters reported that 15 people survived, including 4 who received medical care for injuries; the figures could change as searches continued. Authorities had not determined the cause of the blaze.

The Crow's analysis

The disaster presents both an urgent search-and-rescue challenge and questions about industrial safety. The three-story building suffered structural damage, including sections of collapsed roof, restricting rescuers' access and increasing the risk of further accidents. The missing-person count is an operational estimate, not confirmation of additional deaths. The next crucial step is reconciling lists of workers on site with survivors and identified victims, alongside the investigation into the fire's origin.

Counterpoint

Early accounts differ over how many workers were inside the facility. There is no verified basis to blame electrical faults, refrigeration equipment or negligence before the official investigation concludes. Casualty figures remain preliminary.
