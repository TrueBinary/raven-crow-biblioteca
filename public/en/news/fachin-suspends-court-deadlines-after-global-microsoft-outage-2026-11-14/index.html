Fachin suspends court deadlines after global Microsoft outage

Deadlines on November 12 and 13 were paused, with counting expected to resume on Monday, November 16.

What happened

Edson Fachin, president of Brazil's Supreme Federal Court (STF) and National Council of Justice (CNJ), suspended the counting of procedural deadlines on November 12 and 13, 2026, following disruptions to Microsoft services. Agência Brasil reported that the decision, requested by the Brazilian Bar Association (OAB), applies to the country's judiciary and that deadlines are expected to resume on Monday, November 16. Outlook and Teams were affected, while the electronic adjudication system and other judicial activities remained operational. Microsoft attributed the outage to server problems and said it was unaware of malicious activity.

The Crow's analysis

The suspension aims to prevent procedural harm to lawyers and litigants who rely on digital communications. It also exposes the judiciary's reliance on external commercial platforms for administrative work, even where core case-processing systems remain available.

Counterpoint

When a private service goes down and court deadlines have to stop, the obvious question is where the contingency plan went. The pause protects due process, but the infrastructure needs credible alternatives.
