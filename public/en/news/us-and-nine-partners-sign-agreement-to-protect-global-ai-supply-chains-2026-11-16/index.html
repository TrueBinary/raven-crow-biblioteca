US AND NINE PARTNERS SIGN AGREEMENT TO PROTECT GLOBAL AI SUPPLY CHAINS

Ten countries sign the San Francisco Declaration to safeguard chip supplies, infrastructure and other critical AI resources.

What happened

The United States and nine partners signed the San Francisco Declaration on the Secure Foundations of Enduring Prosperity on November 15, 2026, with the announcement reported on November 16. Signatories are the United States, Australia, India, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Qatar, Singapore, the United Arab Emirates and the United Kingdom. The agreement seeks cooperation to identify and safeguard sensitive technologies and infrastructure underpinning artificial intelligence (AI) supply chains, including semiconductors and critical minerals. Planned working groups cover AI policy, adoption, supply chains, security hubs and resilience. The announcement did not establish binding investment amounts or a comprehensive implementation timetable.

The Crow's analysis

The development treats AI as an industrial and strategic supply chain rather than simply software. Advanced computing depends on chips, power, facilities and raw materials; a disruption at one point can affect the entire system. The declaration builds on Pax Silica, a US-led initiative launched in 2025 to reduce strategic vulnerabilities and risky dependencies. The ten signatories bring different strengths in technology, energy, finance and resources. Brazil is not among the initial signatories, making the initiative relevant alongside new US licensing requirements for advanced chip exports to Brazil, though the declaration itself does not prove Brazilian companies have lost access.

Counterpoint

Signing a political declaration does not immediately establish factories, joint export controls or funded projects. Competition with China provides geopolitical context, but it would be inaccurate to claim the declaration automatically bans trade with Chinese companies. Binding mechanisms, budgets, enforcement and consequences for non-signatories remain unclear.
