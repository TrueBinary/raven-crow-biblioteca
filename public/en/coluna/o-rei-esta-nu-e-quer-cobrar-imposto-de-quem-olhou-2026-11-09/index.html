THE EMPEROR HAS NO CLOTHES. AND NOW HE WANTS TO TAX THOSE WHO NOTICED.

Absurdity no longer hides. It parades in public, demands applause and calls anyone who points it out intolerant.

The Crow Investigator · Raven Crow · 2026-11-09

In 1837, Hans Christian Andersen published The Emperor's New Clothes, a story that feels as if it were written yesterday. Two swindlers persuade a ruler that they have made magnificent clothes invisible to fools and the incompetent. Nobody wants to look foolish: ministers praise them, the crowd applauds, and the emperor marches naked. Then a child states the obvious: the emperor has no clothes! Dear reader, today's emperor is not merely naked. He flaunts his shamelessness, wants to control our homes, families and money, and some people actually defend the damn spectacle.

Pointing out the obvious has become a moral offense. Call something absurd and someone will explain that it is humanity's latest triumph. The emperor is naked? You misunderstand imperial fashion. He abuses power? Consider the context. He taxes the peanut seller? It is for the common good! Soon there will be a Ministry of Invisible Clothing, a Trouser Inspection Department and an emergency levy to buy the emperor underwear. Falsehood no longer asks for silence. It demands applause.

Worse still is how accustomed Brazilians have become to burdens that should spark scrutiny. A worker buying a Renault Kwid, one of the market's simplest cars, pays a price containing substantial taxes. Claims of nearly 40 percent need checking against the model, date and calculation method; I will not present that figure as settled. But why should workers not question taxation on earnings, purchases and ownership? Tiradentes became a symbol of resistance to colonial fiscal oppression, although the Inconfidência Mineira was about more than one tax collection. Today he might need three forms, a notarized signature and a fee just to protest it.

The question goes beyond taxation. Some people treat truth as whatever suits them, attacking the speaker instead of examining the claim. Reality does not depend on my wishes or a vote by a committee of enlightened officials. If I declare myself a chair, four wooden legs will not sprout from my back. This deliberately absurd example is not permission to demean anyone or trivialize complex human questions. It simply shows that freedom does not give us unlimited power over reality. Thomas Aquinas discussed truth as the mind's conformity with what is. Reality need not kneel before our opinions.

Here is what matters most to me. I am a Roman Catholic. I believe in Christ and will not hide my faith merely to avoid discomfort. Christ said, 'I am the way and the truth and the life' (John 14:6), and 'the truth will set you free' (John 8:32). How could I profess that faith and pretend otherwise out of fear? Christian martyrs witnessed to their faith even unto death, and religious persecution persists. Not every disagreement is persecution, nor can one claim without evidence that Christians suffer the most deaths for their faith. Criticism of communism, authoritarianism or attacks on religious freedom is legitimate; love of truth demands arguments as well as conviction.

Before anyone claims I want a world without rules, government or respect for differences: no. Taxes can fund necessary services; people can disagree with me; not every cultural change is decline. And truth must correct me too. The problem begins when coexistence becomes an excuse to silence criticism of abuses, respect becomes compulsory agreement, and power treats citizens as children. Augustine reflected on libido dominandi, the lust for domination. That temptation belongs to no single party. It can wear red, blue, national colors or a perfectly pressed suit.

THE EMPEROR HAS NO CLOTHES! What frightens me is how many have learned to admire garments that do not exist. Dear reader, you may also wonder how people carry on amid such glaring contradictions. But do not let outrage destroy what you hope to defend. Do not turn the pursuit of truth into arrogance or the defense of faith into contempt. Pray. Work hard. Study. Care for your family. Stay close to people you trust. Defend what is good, beautiful and true, and admit your mistakes. May God guide, protect and bless you. The emperor may parade naked and even charge admission. I still will not pretend he is dressed.
